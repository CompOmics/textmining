ABSTRACT:
KA-PARP1 was expressed in HeLa-KO PARP1 cells. Protein targets of KA-PARP1 were determined in cell lysate by using a DTB-modified NAD+analog.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
LC-MS/MS analysis of KA-PARP1 targets in HeLa celly lysate

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Hela KO-PARP1-cells[1] (kindly gifted by the Bürkle group, University of Konstanz) were cultured in Dulbecoo’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). 24 h before the experiment, the cells were seeded in 10 cm dishes (1.5*106cell). Then, the cells were transfected with the GFP-KA-PARP1-vector using the Effectene transfection kit (Qiagen) following the manufacturers’ manual (2.4 ug DNA, and 30 μL Effectene). The transfection mix was removed after 6 h and exchanged with new DMEM. After 26 h, the medium was removed, and the cells were washed with 1 × PBS (4 mL). 1 × PBS (2 mL) was added, the cells were scratched, and the cell suspension was transferred into a 15 mL falcon tube (4 dishes per pellet) and centrifuged (4 °C, 15 min 1000 g). The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was washed with 1 × PBS (2 mL) centrifuged (4°C, 15 min, 1000 g) and the supernatant was removed. The pellets were frozen at -80 °C until further use. As control KO-PARP1 cells (2.5 1.5*106cell) were seeded in a 10 cm dishcm dish and harvested as described above. Pellets were frozen at -80 °C until further use.  The cell pellets were placed on ice and lysis buffer (HEPES, pH 7.4, 25 mM, NaCl 50 mM, MgCl2 5 mM, NP40 1%, TCEP 0.5 mM) was added and the cell pellets were incubated for 15 min on ice before sonication (3 × 5 s). Cell debris was cleared by centrifugation (4 °C, 14000 g, 30 min) and protein concentration was determined via BCA. The protein concentration of the lysate was adjusted to 4.8 mg/ml with lysis buffer and Et-DTB-NAD+(100 M) was added (total volume 100 L). The reaction mixture was incubated on a shaker for 2 h (30 °C, 400 rpm). As controls lysate without KA-PARP1, lysate without Et-DTB-NAD+, and lysate without KA-PARP1 and Et-DTB-NAD+ were used. After 2 h, the reaction was centrifuged on a desk centrifuge and the reaction was transferred to low binding tube, then prewashed streptavidin beads (Pierce, high capacity, 90 L) were added and the lysates were incubated rolling overnight at 4 °C. The next day, the beads were washed and the proteins were eluted as previously described.[2] The elution fractions were lyophilized and frozen at -20 °C until further processing. Subsequently, proteins were proteolyzed following the in-gel digestion protocol from Shevchenko et al.[3] with some modifications as published by Lehner et al.[2] The experiment was performed in duplicates. DATA PROCESSING:
Raw files were analyzed using MaxQuant (version 1.6.1.0)[9] with match between runs and label-free quantification (minimum ratio count 2) enabled. Proteins were identified using the human reference proteome downloaded from the UniProtKB database and for common contamination, the integrated database was used. Further, data were processed with the Perseus software (1.6.15.0) LFQ intensities were log2 transformed and filtered to be detected in at least 2 out of 4 replicates, and missing values were imputed from normal distribution. Identified proteins were filtered for reverse hits and common contaminants. Then, significantly enriched proteins were identified by an ANOVA-based multiple sample test (false discovery rate (FDR) = 0.05, S0 = 0.1), averaged, and normalized by Z-scoring. After a post hoc Turkey’s honest significant difference (HSD test) for one-way ANOVA, enriched proteins were analyzed by hierarchical clustering (Euclidean distance) and visualized as a heat map.
