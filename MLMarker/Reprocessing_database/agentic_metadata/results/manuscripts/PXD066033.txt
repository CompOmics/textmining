ABSTRACT:
Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors have proven their efficacy for treating tumors defective in homologous recombination via synthetic lethality. In response to DNA breaks, PARP1 is the primary ADP-ribosylation writer, modifying itself (auto-modification) and other proteins to facilitate repair. However, enzymatic inhibition blocks both processes, making it difficult to dissect their distinct functional roles. Using proteomics and site-directed mutagenesis, we identified a PARP1 mutant deficient in auto-modification, yet it retains catalytic activity. This separation-of-function mutant revealed that PARP1 auto-modification slows DNA replication fork progression but is dispensable for repair factor recruitment. Instead, auto-modification promotes the timely release of PARP1 at DNA break sites and prevents the formation of replication stress. Simultaneous inhibition of FEN1 and loss of PARP1 auto-modification gives rise to synthetic lethality, implicating auto-modification in Okazaki fragment processing. Our results demonstrate that trapping of PARP at DNA breaks impedes repair factor accessibility, constituting an important dimension of PARP-inhibitor-driven cytotoxicity.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Purification of ADPr-modified PARP1 while preserving ester-linked modifications: To enrich in vivo auto-modified PARP1, U2OS cells (10 cm dishes, 4 replicates) with PARP1 WT, 4SA, or HPF1 KO backgrounds were used. WT and 4SA cells were treated with 0.02% MMS (30 min, 37°C), and HPF1 KO cells with 2 mM H₂O₂ (10 min, 37°C) to activate PARP1. In vitro reactions were performed with 1 µM purified PARP1 WT or E988K, 1 µM damaged DNA, and 1 mM NAD⁺ in ADPr buffer (25 mM HEPES pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 0.5 mM TCEP). HPF1 was added to half of the reactions at equimolar concentration; controls received buffer only.  Cells were washed twice in ice-cold PBS, scraped, pelleted (500 × g, 4°C), and lysed in modified RIPA buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 150 mM NaCl, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, 1% NP-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS) with 2 mM Olaparib, 1 mM ADP-HPD, 750 U/mL Benzonase, and EDTA-free protease inhibitor. In vitro reactions were treated identically. Lysis was for 30 min on ice in 250 µL buffer, followed by dilution with 1 mL buffer lacking MgCl₂ and Benzonase but supplemented with 30 mM EDTA. Lysates were centrifuged (10 min, 16,000 × g, 4°C) and supernatants incubated with 30 µL pre-equilibrated PARP1-nanobody agarose beads (Chromotek) for 1 h at 4°C. Beads were washed 3× in ice-cold buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 0.1 mM DTT), transferred to clean tubes, and split for treatment with 500 nM PARG or mock in PARG buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.2, 50 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl₂) for 30 min at RT.  Stringent washes followed: (i) 2× in ice-cold modified RIPA (as above, with 30 mM EDTA), (ii) 2× in high-salt buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 500 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 0.1 mM DTT), (iii) 2× in low-salt buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 1 mM EDTA, 0.1 mM DTT). Proteins were digested on-bead using Lys-C in 200 µL digestion buffer (1.25 ng/µL Lys-C, 25 mM PIPES pH 6.5, 3 M guanidine, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1 mM TCEP) at 30°C for 1 h. Guanidine increases missed cleavages and improves ETD-based localization by enhancing high-charge-state ions and coverage of lysine-rich regions. Digestion was stopped with 0.5% TFA, and peptides were separated from beads via 0.45 µM filters (Millipore).  Peptides were purified with low-pH StageTips (C18) using 0.1% formic acid for wash/elution; peptides were eluted with 30% acetonitrile in 0.1% formic acid. CAA alkylation was omitted to preserve ester-linked ADPr; reduced thiol groups remain protonated at low pH and do not form disulfide bonds. Eluates were dried by SpeedVac (2 h, 30°C) and resuspended in 11 µL of 0.1% formic acid for MS.  ETD-based mass spectrometric analysis: Samples (datasets 1–3) were analyzed using an Orbitrap Fusion Lumos Tribrid MS and EASY-nLC 1200 with 20-cm columns (75 µm ID, ReproSil-Pur 1.9 µm C18-AQ). Columns were heated to 40°C. Peptides were eluted with gradients of Buffer A (0.1% formic acid) and Buffer B (80% ACN in 0.1% formic acid): 5–15% B (27.5 min), ramp to 30% B (7.5 min), wash (12 min); total 50 min. Electrospray ionization used a Nanospray Flex Ion Source (2 kV, 275°C, 30% RF).  Full MS at 120,000 resolution (300–1750 m/z), AGC target 600,000, max injection 50 ms. Precursors isolated in 1.3 m/z window, AGC 200,000, max injection 250 ms, quadrupole isolation window 2 Da, EThcD at 20 NCE. Selection: ion intensity >110,000; charge state 3–5 (priority order). Dynamic exclusion: 30 s. Fragmented ions analyzed at 60,000 resolution, topN = 5 in DDA mode.

DATA PROCESSING:
Data processing of site-specific ETD-based ADPr data MS raw data were analyzed using MaxQuant (v1.5.3.30). The search space was based on all human proteins and isoforms from UniProt (downloaded on June 25, 2023), supplemented with PARP1 mutant sequences specific to this dataset. Default MaxQuant settings were used with modifications: variable modifications included N-terminal acetylation, Met oxidation, Cys carbamidomethylation, and ADP-ribosylation on C, D, E, H, K, R, S, T, and Y. Enzyme specificity was set to LysC, allowing up to 5 missed cleavages per peptide and a maximum of 4 variable modifications per peptide. Matching between runs (MBR) was enabled, and the delta score threshold for modification-specific peptides was increased to 20. Second peptide search was disabled. The data were filtered using MaxQuant’s posterior error probability to achieve a false discovery rate (FDR) below 1% at both the peptide-spectrum match and protein assignment levels.  Data filtering of PARP1 ADPr sites recorded with ETD fragmentation while preserving ester-linked modifications MaxQuant outputs were further processed to ensure high-confidence site localization and quantification. As experimental conditions can result in different ADPr linkage types (e.g., HPF1-dependent serine ADPr), each experiment was analyzed independently to prevent localization inference across conditions. ADPr sites with localization probability <0.9 were excluded. For multi-modified peptides, each site was considered independently to avoid incorrect intensity attribution.  MBR intensities were only transferred when the exact modified peptide sequence passed the localization threshold and was supported by at least one confidently localized MS/MS event within the same experimental group. This prevents incorrect mapping to isobaric peptides lacking direct evidence. An additional quality filter was applied by discarding ions with MULTI-MATCH scores below 200 to retain only high-confidence identifications.  To normalize for differences in PARP1 expression and sample loading, ADPr site intensities were scaled by adjusting total PARP1 intensity in each experiment to the global median. For comparisons between PARP1 WT and 4SA mutants, peptide-level quantification was used without requiring site-specific localization filtering. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test (p < 0.001).
