ABSTRACT:
Here we made use of a human osteosarcoma U-2 OS modified cell line (U-2 OS DIvA- Flag-GFP-SMC1), expressing a hydroxytamoxifen controlled restriction enzyme (AsiSI-ER which allows the induction of multiple annotated DSBs) and Flag GFP-SMC. We performed tandem purification before and after DSB induction followed by mass spectrometry to identify cohesin interacting partners.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Cohesin interacting partners before and after DSB induction

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
The DIvA-cell line was modified to stably express FLAG-GFP-tagged SMC1 (construct kindly provided by E. Watrin). All steps were carried out in the presence of phosphatase inhibitor (PhosSTOP, Sigma) and protease inhibitor (Roche), used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. To obtain nuclear extracts, cells were resuspended in buffer A (10 mM Tris HCl pH7.5, 10 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 0.7% Triton, 1 mM DTT, Protease inhibitor, Phosphatase inhibitor) and incubated for 5 min at 4°C on a rotating wheel. Cell were spun at 1500 g for 5 min. The pellet containing chromatin and cell debris were resuspended into buffer B (10 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 200 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 0.1% triton, 0.5% NP-40, 1 mM DTT, 5 mM of CaCl2 and 20 U of MNase) and incubated for 25 min at room temperature on a rotating wheel. MNase digestion was stopped by the addition of 2 mM EGTA. This S2 fraction corresponds to solubilized chromatin-bound proteins was then centrifuged at 20000g for 20 min at 4°C. The supernatant was used for the co-immunoprecipitation after an initial preclearing with protein A agarose beads (Thermo scientific, 20334). To obtain nucleolar extracts, cells were centrifuged at 800 g for 5 min. Cells were resuspended in buffer C (350 mM sucrose, 15 mM NaCl, 10 mM MgCl2, 10 mM Tris pH 7.5, 1 mM DTT) and sonicated twice for 30 sec (Bioruptor). After a 2 min centrifugation at 2000 g the pellet was washed in Buffer C and added to an equal volume of buffer D (880mM Sucrose, 1 mM MgCl2, 10 mM Tris pH 7.5, 1 mM DTT). The sucrose cushion was then centrifuged at 2000g for 5 min. For solubilisation, the pellet was resuspended in buffer A and incubated for 25 min at room temperature on a rotating wheel before the addition of 2 mM EGTA to stop the MNase digestion. The supernatant containing the nucleolar extract was kept after a centrifugation at 20000g for 20 min. For both nuclear and nucleolar extracts, a FLAG IP was first carried out using FLAG-M2 beads (A2220, Sigma) added for 2 hr on a rotating wheel at 4°C. This was followed by three washes in buffer C (10 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 200 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 0.1% triton, 0.25% NP40, 1 mM DTT) before FLAG peptide (F3290, Sigma) elution (10 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 100 µg/ml FLAG peptide) for 30 min at 4°C. This first immunoprecipitated extract was secondly subjected to a GFP IP by incubation with GFP-Trap magnetic beads (gtm-20, Chromotek) overnight at 4°C. Three washes with buffer C were performed before elution with 2x SDS loading buffer. Protein extracts were migrated on a SDS-PAGE gel, and Coomassie stained. The excised band was subjected to mass spectrometry (IPBS mass spectrometry facility, Toulouse Mass spectrometry analysis The trypsic peptides were analysed by nano‐liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) using an UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano system (Dionex, Amsterdam, The Netherlands) coupled to an OrbiTrap Velos mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, Bremen, Germany). Five microliters of each sample was loaded onto a C18 pre‐column (300 μm id, 5 mm; Dionex), at 20 μL/min, in 5% acetonitrile and 0.05 W% trifluoroacetic acid. After 5 min of desalting, the pre‐column was switched on line with the analytical C18 column (75 μm id × 15 cm C18 column; packed in‐house with ReproSil‐Pur C18‐AQ 3 μm resin, Dr Maisch; Proxeon Biosystems), equilibrated in 95% solvent A (5% acetonitrile and 0.2% formic acid) and 5% solvent B (80% acetonitrile and 0.2% formic acid). Peptides were eluted using a 5–50% gradient of solvent B over 120 min and at a flow rate of 300 nL/min. The LTQ-Orbitrap was operated in data dependent acquisition mode with the Xcalibur software. Survey scan MS were acquired in the Orbitrap on the 300–2000 m/z range with the resolution set to a value of 60,000. The 20 most intense ions per survey scan were selected for CID fragmentation and the resulting fragments were analysed in the linear trap (LTQ). Dynamic exclusion was employed within 60 s to prevent repetitive selection of the same peptide.

DATA PROCESSING:
Raw MS files were processed with the Mascot software for database search and Proline for label-free quantitative analysis Data were searched against Human entries of the UniProtKB protein database (release SwissProt 20160129 , 20,198 entries). Carbamidomethylation of cysteines was set as a fixed modification, whereas oxidation of methionine was set as variable modifications. Specificity of trypsin/P digestion was set for cleavage after K or R, and two missed trypsin cleavage sites were allowed. The mass tolerance was set to 10 ppm for the precursor and to 20 mmu in tandem MS mode. Minimum peptide length was set to 7 amino acids, and identification results were further validated in Proline by the target decoy approach using a reverse database at both a PSM and protein false-discovery rate of 1%. For label-free relative quantification of the proteins across biological replicates and conditions, cross-assignment of peptide ions peaks was enabled inside group with a match time window of 1 min, after alignment of the runs with a tolerance of +/- 600s
