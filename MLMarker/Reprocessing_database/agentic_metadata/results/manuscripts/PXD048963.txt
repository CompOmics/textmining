ABSTRACT:
Genetic aberrations of the maternal UBE3A allele, which encodes the E3 ubiquitin ligase E6AP, are the cause of Angelman syndrome (AS), an imprinting disorder. In most cases, the maternal UBE3A allele is not expressed. Yet, approximately 10 percent of AS individuals harbor distinct point mutations in the maternal allele resulting in the expression of full-length E6AP variants that frequently display compromised ligase activity. In a high-throughput screen, we identified cyanocobalamin, a vitamin B12-derivative, and several alloxazine derivatives as activators of the AS-linked E6AP-F583S variant. Furthermore, we show by cross-linking coupled to mass spectrometry that cobalamins affect the structural dynamics of E6AP-F583S and apply limited proteolysis coupled to mass spectrometry to obtain information about the regions of E6AP that are involved in, or are affected by, binding cobalamins and alloxazine derivatives. Our data suggest that dietary supplementation with vitamin B12 could  be beneficial for AS individuals.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Cobalamins function as allosteric activators of an Angelman syndrome-associated UBE3A/E6AP variant

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Experiments were performed essentially as described in Offensperger et al., 2020. For analysis, approximately 100 µg of wild-type E6AP or E6AP-F583S were cross-linked in the absence or presence of 100 µM cyanocobalamin or 1.5-molar excess of GST-16 E6.  Limited proteolysis coupled to mass spectrometry (LiP-MS) experiments were performed essentially as described by the Picotti lab (Schopper et al., 2017, Cappelletti et al. 2021, Piazza et al., 2018).  In short, 100 µg of E6AP (wt or F583S) were incubated with either DMSO, 100 µM cyanocobalamin, N12, I08 or 1.5 molar excess of GST-16 E6 for 30 min on ice and equilibrated to 25 °C prior to LiP. For LiP, 5 µL of 0.2 µg/µL Proteinase K (from Engydontium album, enzyme:substrate (E:S) ratio 1:100) was added and samples were incubated at 25 °C for 1 min before the enzymatic digestion was stopped by heating of samples to 98 °C for 5 min. After cooling to room temperature, sodium deoxycholate (SOD) was added to a final concentration of 5 % (w/v).Proteins were reduced with 2.5 mM TCEP for 30 min at 37 °C, alkylated with 5 mM iodoacetamide for 30 min at 23 °C in the dark. After dilution of SOD to 1 %, trypsin (Promega V5113, Sequencing grade) was added at an E:S ratio of 1:40 and incubated for 18 h at 37 °C. The enzymatic digestion was stopped, and SOD precipitated by addition of 98  % formic acid (FA) to a final concentration of 2 %. The SOD precipitate was removed by centrifugation at 16 000 g for 10 min and remaining detergent extracted by HiPPR detergent removal columns (Thermo Scientific, #88305) before peptides were desalted with C18 Spin tips (Pierce #84850). For MS analysis, dried peptides were resuspended in 5 % acetonitrile (ACN), 0.1 % FA. For retention time normalization, iRT peptides (Biognosys) were added at a ratio of 1:40. ~500 ng peptides were loaded onto a 15 cm Acclaim PepMap C18 column (P/N 164943, Thermo Scientific) connected to an EASY-nLC™ 1200 system and separated across a 75 min gradient. Mass spectra were acquired on a QExactive HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) in data-independent mode using 22 variable windows that were adapted to the m/z density distribution of ions in the samples.7 Precursor mass spectra were acquired in the Orbitrap at 120K over a mass range of 300-1650 m/z using 60 ms maximum injection time (max IT) and an automatic gain control (AGC) target of 3e6. Fragment mass spectra were acquired in the Orbitrap at 30K with an AGC target of 1e6 while max IT was set to auto. Precursors were fragmented with stepped HCD at 25.5, 27, 30 %.

DATA PROCESSING:
Raw data was analysed with Spectronaut version 18.4.231017.55695 in directDIA mode. BGS Factory settings were used with the exception that digest type was set to unspecific, min peptide length was set to 6, and background imputation was applied within the DIA quantification tab. The unpaired t-test results on the peptide level were exported for further analysis, data transformation and visualization using python packages pandas (version 1.4.4, https://pandas.pydata.org/), numpy (version 1.21.5, https://www.numpy.org) and plotly express (plotly version 5.9.0, https://plotly.com/python/plotly-express/) running on python (version 3.9.13). The sequence plot code was adapted from Schessner et al., 2022.
