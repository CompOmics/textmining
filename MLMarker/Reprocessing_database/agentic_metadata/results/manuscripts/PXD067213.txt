ABSTRACT:
This study employs a factorial design to delineate the combinatorial effects of cardiac-specific conditional gene knockout and pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy via transverse aortic constriction (TAC). Four experimental cohorts were established: (1) conditional knockout mice subjected to TAC (CKO_TAC), (2) knockout controls with Sham surgery (CKO_Sham), (3) floxed control mice receiving TAC (ff_TAC), and (4) floxed Sham-operated controls (ff_Sham). Following a 5-week pressure overload (TAC), myocardial tissue underwent parallel proteomic and phosphoproteomic profiling using TMT-based quantitative mass spectrometry. This dual-omics approach specifically interrogates: (a) the intrinsic genetic perturbation effect (CKO_ Sham vs ff_ Sham), (b) the pure pressure overload response (ff_TAC vs ff_Sham), and critically, (c) the genotype-by-environment interaction through synergistic/antagonistic divergence between observed versus predicted additive effects in CKO_TAC relative to genetic and surgical controls. Phosphoproteomic data further resolve signaling pathway dynamics underlying phenotypic modulation.

Proteomic and Phosphoproteomic Profiling of Heart in Pak2cko Mice Under Pressure Overload

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
1. Sample Preparation Cardiac tissue was pulverized in liquid nitrogen and homogenized in ice-cold lysis buffer (8 M urea, 2% SDS, 100 mM NH₄HCO₃, pH 8.0) with protease/phosphatase inhibitors (Pierce™ Halt Cocktail + 10 mM NaF/1 mM Na₃VO₄). Homogenates were sonicated (3 × 10 s pulses, 30% amplitude), centrifuged (12,000 × g, 4°C, 15 min), and supernatants reduced with 10 mM DTT (56°C, 1 h) and alkylated with 50 mM iodoacetamide (RT, dark, 1 h). Proteins were acetone-precipitated (-20°C, 2 h), washed with 90% acetone, and dissolved in 6 M urea/100 mM TEAB (pH 8.5). After BCA quantification, 100 μg aliquots were buffer-exchanged to 100 mM NH₄HCO₃ (10-kDa filters) and digested with trypsin (1:50 w/w, 37°C, 16 h). For phosphoproteomics, tissue was minced in liquid nitrogen and lysed (100 mM NH₄HCO₃, 6 M urea, 0.2% SDS, pH 8.0). Lysates were sonicated (5 min, ice), centrifuged (12,000 × g, 4°C, 15 min), and supernatants quantified (Bradford assay). Proteins were reduced (2 mM DTT, 56°C, 1 h), alkylated (30 mM iodoacetamide, RT, dark, 1 h), acetone-precipitated (-20°C, ≥2 h), and dissolved in 0.1 M TEAB/6 M urea. After requantification, 5 mg protein was digested with Trypsin Gold (1:50, 37°C, 16 h), desalted (C18 cartridge), and dried. 2. TMT Labeling 120 μg protein/sample was digested with trypsin (1.5 μg, 100 mM TEAB, 37°C, 4 h + overnight). Digests were acidified (formic acid, pH < 3), centrifuged (12,000 × g, 5 min), desalted (C18 columns), and lyophilized. Peptides were labeled with TMT reagent (41 μL in acetonitrile, RT, 2 h), quenched with 8% ammonia, pooled, desalted, and lyophilized. 3. Fractionation Peptides were separated by high-pH reversed-phase HPLC (Waters BEH C18 column, 4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm, 50°C) using mobile phase A (2% acetonitrile, pH 10.0) and B (98% acetonitrile). A 1-min interval collection yielded 10 pooled fractions, lyophilized and reconstituted in 0.1% formic acid. 4. Phosphopeptide Enrichment Fractions were resuspended in 250 mM acetic acid/30% acetonitrile (pH 2.5–3.0) and enriched using Phos-Select Iron Affinity Gel (Sigma-Aldrich, P9740) per manufacturer's protocol. 5.LC-MS/MS Analysis Proteomics: 1 μg of peptides was analyzed on an EASY-nLC™ 1200 UHPLC coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X (DDA mode). Separation used a C18 Nano-Trap column (2 cm × 75 μm, 3 μm) and analytical column (15 cm × 150 μm, 1.9 μm). MS settings: spray voltage 2.3 kV, capillary 320°C, full MS (350–1500 m/z, 60,000 resolution, AGC 3×10⁶, 20 ms max injection). Top 40 precursors were HCD-fragmented (45,000 resolution, AGC 5×10⁴, 86 ms max injection, 32% NCE). Phosphoproteomics: 1 μg peptides were separated over 120 min (5–90% B; A: 0.1% FA; B: 0.1% FA/80% acetonitrile). MS/MS: 15,000 resolution, 80 ms max injection, 27% NCE, dynamic exclusion 30 s.

DATA PROCESSING:
MS/MS spectra from individual runs were independently searched against the Mus_musculus_uniprot_2020_7_2.fasta(86555 sequences) database using Proteome Discoverer 2.4 (PD 2.4, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Search parameters specified: precursor mass tolerance 10 ppm, fragment mass tolerance 0.02 Da, carbamidomethylation (C) as fixed modification, methionine oxidation and TMT plex labeling as dynamic modifications, and N-terminal acetylation plus TMT plex labeling as variable modifications. A maximum of two missed cleavages was permitted. To ensure high-confidence identifications, PD 2.4 filtered results using the following criteria: Peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) were validated at a false discovery rate (FDR) ≤1% (q-value <0.01) with a minimum peptide confidence threshold corresponding to >99% probability. Proteins required ≥1 unique peptide. The protein quantitation results were statistically analyzed using the Student's t-test. Proteins exhibiting significant differences (p < 0.05) with |FC| > 1.2 between experimental and control groups were defined as differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). For phosphoproteomics data, high-pH fractionated MS/MS spectra were independently searched against the same database using Proteome Discoverer 2.2 (PD 2.2, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Search parameters included: 10 ppm precursor mass tolerance; 0.02 Da fragment mass tolerance; carbamidomethylation (C) as fixed modification; variable modifications including methionine oxidation, serine/threonine/tyrosine phosphorylation (S/T/Y), and N-terminal acetylation; with ≤2 missed cleavages permitted. Proteins were identified at ≤1% FDR (peptide and protein level), requiring ≥1 unique peptide. Indistinguishable proteins sharing peptides were grouped. Label-free quantification (LFQ) uses precursor intensity values. Differential expression was assessed using Mann-Whitney U tests (p<0.05). Phosphoproteins meeting identical significance thresholds (p < 0.05 and |FC| >1.2 were classified as DEPs.
