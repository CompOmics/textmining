ABSTRACT:
Cellular stress responses are crucial for survival in sub-optimal conditions, and almost invar-iably involve proteome-wide alterations at the levels of protein abundance and post-translational modification with poly-ubiquitin chains (poly-ubiquitylation). However, most of our understanding of human cell stress responses at the protein level stems from experiments in rapidly-dividing cell culture models. Here, we used the A549 human diploid lung adenocarci-noma cell line (alveolar, type-2) in proliferating or bleomycin-induced senescent states, to ex-plore their responses to a severe proteotoxic stress with the proteasome inhibitor bortezomib (100 nM for 24 hours) at the total and poly-ubiquitylated proteome levels.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Effect of proteasome inhibition on total and poly-ubiquitylated proteomes of human A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells in different proliferation states

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
For total proteome sample preparation, washed cells were lysed in ~4 pellet volumes of ice-cold denaturing lysis buffer (50 mM HEPES pH 8.0, 8 M urea, 1 % SDS, 1 % Triton X-100, 1 % IGEPAL CA-630, 1 % Tween 20, 1 % sodium deoxycholate, and 150 mM NaCl) supplemented with 1 x Roche cOmplete EDTA-free Protease Inhibitor Cocktail, 1 mM PMSF, 40 mM 2-chloroacetamide, 10 µM PR-619, and 20 U/µL Pierce Universal Nucleases. Lysates were sonicated, and cleared (16,000 g, 4 °C, 5 min). Total protein concentration was normalised based on BCA assay estimate. 50 µg of protein was reduced and alkylated with 10 mM TCEP (5 min, 80 °C) and 40 mM 2-chloroacetamide (15 min, room temperature) and peptides prepared by SP4, as described previously (doi: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c04200). Briefly, 4 volumes of 2.5 mg/mL glass beads in acetonitrile were added to lysates and vortexed (5 s at 400 rpm) to precipitate proteins. The precipitates were pelleted (5 min, 16,000 g) and carefully washed 3x with 80 % ethanol without resuspension (with 2 min at 16,000 g in between each wash). The pelleted precipitate was digested in a Thermomixer Comfort (18 h, 1,000 rpm, 37 °C) with sequencing-grade modified trypsin (Promega, Cat# V5111) at 1:100 enzyme:protein ratio in 50 mM HEPES pH 8.0, before proceeding to TMT labelling. For poly-ubiquitylome sample preparation, 100 μg of cell lysate were reduced and alkylated (10 mM TCEP and 20 mM 2-chloroacetamide), and diluted in IP buffer.  Magne HaloTag beads (Promega, Cat# G7281) conjugated to a custom-made trypsin-resistant Tandem-Ubiquitin-Binding-Entity (trTUBE) with an N-terminal HaloTag separated by a tobacco-etch-virus protease cleavage site (HaloTag—[tev]—trTUBE). Mixtures were incubated for 18 h at 4 °C, with end-over-end rotation at 20 rpm. Beads were then washed 3x with 1 mL of wash buffer and 3x with 1 mL 50 mM HEPES pH 8, before elution of bound proteins via TEV protease-mediated cleavage of the trTUBE. The eluate was digested with 50 ng of sequencing-grade modified trypsin (2 h, 1,000 rpm, 47 °C). Digested peptides were labelled with TMTpro 18-plex in 50 mM HEPES pH 8.0, quenched with 0.4 % hydroxylamine (15 min, room temperature), pooled and dried by vacuum concentration. Dried peptides were resuspended in a solution of 2 % acetonitrile and 0.1 % triethylamine in LC-MS-grade water, and fractionated with a Pierce High pH Reversed-Phase Peptide Fractionation Kit (Thermo Scientific, Cat# 84868), according to the manufacturer’s instructions, adapted to required fraction numbers. Peptides were dried and resolubilised in 5 % acetonitrile and 0.1 % formic acid in LC-MS-grade water, for LC-MS injection. For total proteome digests, 10 µg of peptides were labelled with 50 µg of TMTpro reagent, separated into 12 peptide fractions, and 4 µL of the 30 µL from each fraction analysed by LC-MS. For poly-ubiquitylome digests, all peptide material was labelled with 25 µg of TMTpro reagent, separated into 4 peptide fractions, and 6 µL of the 15 µL from each fraction analysed by LC-MS.

DATA PROCESSING:
LC-MS analysis. TMT-labelled high-pH peptide fractions were analysed by Orbitrap Eclipse MS (Thermo Scientific) with on-line separation on a reversed-phase nanoLC column (450 mm × 0.075 mm ID) packed with ReprosilPur C18AQ (Dr Maisch, 3 μm particles). A 180 min gradient of 4–35% ACN, 0.1% FA at 300 nL/min was delivered via a Dionex UltiMate 3000 nanoHPLC system. Mass spectra were acquired in SPS MS3 mode and FAIMS (-45, -55, -65, and -75 V) with the following settings: MS1 —120k resolution, max IT 50 ms, AGC target 400,000; MS2 — IW 0.7 CID fragmentation, CE 35%, max IT 35 ms, ion trap rapid scan rate, AGC target 10,000; MS3 reporter quantitation — 10 2 m/z notches, HCD fragmentation, CE 55%, 50k resolution, max IT 86 ms, AGC target 100,000. Processing of raw spectrum files. Thermo raw spectrum files were processed with Proteome Discoverer (Thermo Scientific, v. 2.5.0.400) using Sequest HT (2.0.0.24, x64), Percolator (3.05.0), and IMP-ptmRS (1.0.0.0), searching against UniProt Human Swissprot database (UniProtKB 2023-06), supplemented with sequences for common contaminants (doi: 10.1021/acs.jproteome.2c00145), TEV protease, and HaloTag—[tev]—trTUBE6. In order to simplify protein grouping of peptides belonging to ubiquitin, the polyubiquitin-B gene (UBB; P0CG47) was replaced with a single tandem di-ubiquitin sequence, and all other ubiquitin sequences (UBC, and N-terminal of UBA52 and RPS27A) within the database were removede. The settings used for analysis were as follows: enzymes set as Trypsin/P, with a maximum of two missed cleavages; fixed modification was carbamidomethyl (Cys) and TMTpro (Lys, peptide N-term); variable modifications were acetyl, Met-loss, or Met-loss+acetyl (protein N-term), Oxidation (Met). For poly-ubiquitylomics, TMTpro (Lys) and GlyGly+TMTpro (Lys) were set as variable modifications. Mass tolerances were set to 10 ppm (MS1, FTMS), 0.5 Da (MS2, ITMS), and 20 ppm (MS3, FTMS); false discovery rate (FDR) for both protein and peptide identifications was 0.01. For quantitation filtering, a co-isolation threshold of 50 %, average reporter ion signal/noise >= 5, and SPS matches >= 50 % were applied.
