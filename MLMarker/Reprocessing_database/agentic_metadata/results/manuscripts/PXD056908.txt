keywords: Proteomics; Over-labelling; Tmt-labeling; Phosphoproteomics

ABSTRACT:
Comparing the performance of methylamine and hydroxylamine on phospho-peptide analysis, particularly in relation to the identification of more complex dose-dependent patterns.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Efficient removal of TMT-labelling by-products in proteomic samples by methylamine  

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
1. Drug treatment in dose-dependent manner. 2. Digestion 3. TMT-labeling 4. Phospho-enrichement 5. Fractionation 6. LC-MS

DATA PROCESSING:
MS raw files were searched with MaxQuant software (version 1.6.17.0) using the UniProtKB human as reference list for in-silico digest. Default settings were used unless stated specifically. Trypsin was selected as protease and less than 3 missed cleavages were permitted for the phosphoproteome research. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine was set as a fixed modification, meanwhile oxidation of methionine and N-terminal protein acetylation were set as variable modifications. Phosphorylation of serine, threonine and tyrosine was set as variable modifications. Up to 6 modifications were allowed on peptides and the peptide length was restricted within the range of 6-25 amino acids. FDR was set as 1% on protein- and PSM- level. Isotopic impurities in the TMT batch were accounted for by configuring the TMT modifications, allowing MaxQuant to automatically adjust and correct the TMT intensities.
