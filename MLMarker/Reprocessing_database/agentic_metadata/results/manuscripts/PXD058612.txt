ABSTRACT:
Myokines released by skeletal muscle in response to contraction may contribute to the health-promoting effects of exercise. Previous studies with cultured rodent and human myotubes have revealed highly complex patterns of myokine secretion. However, the commonalities and differences in the secretory response of the different cell models have not been explored, limiting the interpretation of these results. In the present study, we performed a comprehensive analysis of contraction-regulated secretomes using the most commonly used skeletal muscle cell models.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
High-resolution analyses of the secretomes from primary human skeletal muscle cells

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Electrical pulse stimulation: On day six of the differentiation, human myotubes (HSkMCs) were washed three times with Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (DPBS) and medium was changed to serum- and phenol-free Dulbecco´s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM 1x). Myotubes were stimulated in serum-free medium with EPS (C-Pace EP, IonOptix LLC) for six hours with 2 ms pulses at 40 V. Control cells, also exposed to electrodes but not connected to the power supply, were simultaneously serum-starved for six hours. Conditioned media (CM) of stimulated and unstimulated cells was collected and centrifuged at 1,000 x g for 10 min at 4°C. Eventually, the cells were washed once with cold DPBS and lyzed either in denaturing SDS buffer (100 mM Tris-HCl, 4% SDS and 100 mM DTT, supplemented with cOmplete™ (Roche)) for proteome analysis. All samples were stored at -80°C until further analysis.  Secretome analysis: For proteomic profiling of the CM of EPS-treated primary HSkMCs, a maximum of 25 µg protein was loaded onto a sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide (SDS-PAGE) gel (10% polyacrylamide, 0.5 cm separation distance) and subjected to in gel protein digestion. For MS-based analyses, lyophilized peptides were reconstituted in 1% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) (v/v) and separated by liquid chromatography (LC) (UltiMateTM 3000), connected to an Orbitrap ExplorisTM 480 mass spectrometer. Peptides were trapped and desalted on an Acclaim™ PepMap™ C18-LC-column (ID: 75 μm, 2 cm length), followed by separation on an Aurora C18 column (AUR2-25075C18A, 25 cm x 75 μm C18 1.6µm), using a two hours three-step gradient. MS data were acquired in data dependent acquisition (DDA) mode at 120,000 resolution (2 s cycle time), m/z range of 350-1,200 and a normalized automatic gain control (AGC) target value of 300%. Fragmentation precursor selection filters were set to charge state between two and six and dynamic exclusion of 45 seconds. Fragmentation of precursors was done with an isolation window (m/z) of 1.6, higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) energy of 30% at 15,000 resolution with automatic adjustment of AGC target value and injection time. Proteome analysis: Cell lysates from unstimulated primary HSkMCs were further solubilized in denaturing SDS buffer (100 mM Tris-HCl, 4% SDS and 100 mM DTT, supplemented with CompleteTM (Roche)), by 10 strokes through an insulin syringe (needle 26 gauge), followed by sonification (two times pulse 0.09 sec_10 sec) for proteome analyses. After centrifugation at 75,000 x g for 30 min at 4°C the supernatants were transferred to new reaction tubes. Protease digestion was performed according to the filter-aided sample preparation (FASP) procedure. Shortly, protein samples (100 µg) were incubated at 96°C for 10 min and then diluted 1:10 (v/v) with urea buffer (UB: 8M urea, 100 mM Tris-HCl pH 8). Samples were then centrifuged at 10,000 x g using centrifugal filter devices (Amicon® Ultra-0.5, P/N UFC5030) and alkylated by incubation with 50 mM iodoacetamide for 15 min at room temperature. Washing steps with urea buffer were repeated three times, protein lysates were digested using Trypsin/Lys-C mixture (Promega) in a 1:25 (w/w) enzyme/protein ratio overnight at 37°C. The next day, filter devices were centrifuged and the peptides were collected and acidified with TFA to a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v). Purification was obtained using C18 solid phase extraction (Strata C18-E, 200mg/ 1ml), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Eluates were lyophilized and stored as aliquots at -80°C. For LC-MS/MS analyses, lyophilized peptides were reconstituted in 1% TFA (v/v) and peptide concentrations were determined. Samples (400 ng) were run in triplicates by LC (UltiMateTM 3000) as described above. MS analysis was performed on an Orbitrap FusionTM LumosTM mass spectrometer coupled to a Nanospray FlexTM ion source and equipped with a high-field asymmetric waveform ion mobility spectrometry (FAIMS Pro) interface. MS-data were obtained in DDA mode using FAIMS compensation voltages (CV) of -40, -60 and -80 V. MS spectra were acquired at 120,000 resolution (3 s cycle time) and m/z range of 350-1,600. Fragmentation precursor selection filters were set to charge state between two and seven, dynamic exclusion of 30 seconds and intensity threshold of 2.5E4. Fragmentation of precursors was done with an isolation window (m/z) 3.6, HCD energy of 30% at 15,000 resolution with automatic adjustment of AGC target value and injection time.

DATA PROCESSING:
Proteome DiscovererTM (PDTM) 3.0 software (ThermoFisher Scientific) was used to analyze the MS raw files. For spectral recalibration, the SpectrumRC node was used with the FASTA database (UniProtKB database, reviewed SwissProt, Homo sapiens TaxID=9606, v2022-12-14 and v2023-06-28). The minora feature detector node was used for quantification with default settings (minimum trace length 5, max. delta RT of isotope pattern multiplets of 0.2 min and for feature to ID linking use only high confident peptide spectrum matches (PSMs). CHIMERYS search was performed against UniProtKB database (reviewed SwissProt, Homo sapiens TaxID=9606, v2022-12-14 and v2023-06-28). Enzymatic digest was conducted using trypsin, allowing a maximum of two missed cleavage sites, b and y ions were selected for HCD fragmentation with a fragment mass tolerance of 0.02 Da. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine was set as static modification, while N-terminal acetylation, N-terminal methionine loss, N-terminal methionine loss/acetylation and methionine oxidation were allowed as variable modifications. Percolator (included in PDTM, max delta Cn: 0.01) was applied for data validation. Label-free quantification was performed on precursor intensities present in at least 20% of replicate features.
