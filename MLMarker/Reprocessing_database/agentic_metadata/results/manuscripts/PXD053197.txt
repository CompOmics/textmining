ABSTRACT:
Accurate chromosome segregation requires the attachment of spindle microtubules to centromeres, which are epigenetically defined by the enrichment of CENP-A nucleosomes. During DNA replication, existing CENP-A nucleosomes undergo dilution as they get redistributed among the two DNA strands. To preserve centromere identity, CENP-A levels must be restored in a cell-cycle controlled manner orchestrated by the Mis18 complex. Here we provide a comprehensive mechanistic basis for PLK1-mediated licensing of CENP-A loading. We demonstrate that PLK1 interacts with Mis18α and Mis18BP1 subunits of the Mis18 complex by recognising self-primed phosphorylations of Mis18α (S54) and Mis18BP1 (T78 and S93) through its Polo-box binding domain. Disrupting these PLK1 phosphorylations perturbed the centromere recruitment of HJURP and new CENP-A loading. Biochemical and functional analyses show that phosphorylation of Mis18α and subsequent PLK1 binding is required to activate the Mis18α/β complex for robust Mis18α/β-HJURP interaction. Thus, our study reveals key molecular events underpinning the licensing role of PLK1 in ensuring accurate centromere inheritance.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Following digestion, samples were diluted with equal volume of 0.1% TFA and spun onto StageTips as described by Rappsilber et al (2007). Peptides were eluted in 40 μL of 80% acetonitrile in 0.1% TFA and concentrated down to 1 μL by vacuum centrifugation (Concentrator 5301, Eppendorf, UK). The peptide samples were then prepared for LC-MS/MS analysis by diluting each one of them to 5 μL by 0.1% TFA. LC-MS analyses were performed on an Orbitrap Fusion™ Lumos™ Tribrid™ Mass Spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, UK) both coupled on-line, to an Ultimate 3000 HPLC (Dionex, Thermo Fisher Scientific, UK). Peptides were separated on a 50 cm (2 µm particle size) EASY-Spray column (Thermo Scientific, UK), which was assembled on an EASY-Spray source (Thermo Scientific, UK) and operated constantly at 55oC. Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in LC-MS grade water and mobile phase B consisted of 80% acetonitrile and 0.1% formic acid. Peptides were loaded onto the column at a flow rate of 0.3 μL min-1 and eluted at a flow rate of 0.25 μL min-1 according to the following gradient: 2 to 40% mobile phase B in 150 min and then to 95% in 11 min. Mobile phase B was retained at 95% for 5 min and returned back to 2% a minute after until the end of the run (190 min).  Survey scans were recorded at 120,000 resolution (scan range 350-1750 m/z), with RF lens of 60%. The Automatic Gain Control (AGC) was set to standard and the maximum injection time at 50ms. MS2 was performed in the orbitrap, with resolution of 50,000. The isolation window was set to 1.6 m/z, the AGC to standard, and the maximum injection time to dynamic. The HCD fragmentation (Olsen et al, 2007) with normalized collision energy was set to 30. Only ions with charge between 3 and 8 were selected for MS2. Dynamic exclusion was set at 60 s.

DATA PROCESSING:
Peak lists were generated with ProteoWizard ver. 3.0.24031 (Chambers et al, 2012).Cross-linked peptides were matched to spectra using Xi software (version 1.7.6.4; Mendes et al., 2018) with in-search assignment of monoisotopic peaks (Lenz et al., 2018). MS1 accuracy was set to 3 ppm and MS2 accuracy to 10 ppm. Trypsin was the protease of choice allowing four missed cleavages and EDC was chosen from the crosslinker list. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine was chosen as a fixed modification and oxidation of methionine, phosphorylation of serine, threonine and tyrosine were chosen as variable modifications.  TR False discovery rate was computed using XiFDR and results reported at 5% residue level false discovery rate (Fischer and Rappsilber, 2017). Our in-house protein database was used for the searches.
