ABSTRACT:
Mitochondria fullfill central functions in cellular metabolism and energy supply. They express their own genome, which encodes key subunits of the oxidative phosphorylation system. However, central mechanisms underlying mitochondrial gene expression remain enigmatic. A lack of suitable technologies to target mitochondrial protein synthesis in living cells has limited experimental access. Here, we silence the translation of specific mitochondrial mRNAs in living cells by delivering synthetic peptide-morpholino chimeras. This approach allows comprehensive temporal monitoring of cellular responses. Our study provides insights into mitochondrial translation, its integration into cellular physiology, and represents an innovative strategy to address mitochondrial gene expression in living cells. The tools that we present can readily be applied to analyze the mechanisms and pathophysiology of mitochondrial gene expression in a broad range of cellular model systems.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Silencing mitochondrial gene expression in living cells – FLAG IPs

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
ZNF703, TMEM186, and SMIM26 complexes were affinity-purified from mitochondria-enriched fractions generated from HEK293T cells expressing FLAG-tagged versions of these proteins. HEK293T wildtype cells were used as control. Mitochondrial fractions were obtained as follows: HEK293T cell pellets were resuspended in THE buffer (10 mM HEPES/KOH, 300 mM Trehalose, 10 mM KCl, 1 mM EGTA, pH 7.4) supplemented with 1% BSA and 1 mM PMSF, and incubated for 10 min on ice. The suspensions were homogenized using a teflon-glass homogenizer by strokes at 800 rpm. Cell debris and unbroken cells were removed by centrifugation, first at 400 x g (10 min, 4°C), and then at 800 x g (10 min, 4°C). Mitochondria were collected by centrifugation at 11,000 x g (10 min, 4°C). The supernatants were discarded, and the mitochondrial pellets were washed by resuspension in THE buffer. Immunoprecipitation of FLAG-tagged proteins: Mitochondria were solubilized at 1 mg/mL protein concentration in lysis buffer (50 mM Tris/HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 10 mM MgCl2, 1% Digitonin, 1 mM PMSF, 1x complete protease inhibitor cocktail) for 30 min on ice with periodic mixing. Insoluble material was removed by centrifugation at 10,000 x g (10 min, 4°C). The lysate was then mixed with anti-FLAG M2 Affinity Gel (Sigma-Aldrich) and incubated for one hour at 4°C. The unbound fraction was removed and the resin washed with washing buffer (50 mM Tris/HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 10 mM MgCl2, 0.1% Digitonin, 1 mM PMSF, 1x complete protease inhibitor cocktail). The native protein complexes were eluted with 0.4 mg/mL FLAG peptide in washing buffer for 30 min at 4°C. LC-MS sample preparation: Proteins of ZNF703-FLAG, TMEM186-FLAG, SMIM26-FLAG and control immunoprecipitation experiments (n=3 each) were separated by SDS-PAGE. Following colloidal Coomassie Blue staining, gel lanes were cut into five pieces each and processed for LC-MS analysis as described before (Peikert et al. 2017, PMID 28485388). Prior to LC-MS analysis, peptides of slices number 1, 2 and 4 were combined. Peptide mixtures were desalted using StageTips (Rappsilber et al. 2007, Nature Protocols 2) and analyzed on a Q Exactive Plus instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

DATA PROCESSING:
Mass spectrometric raw data were processed using MaxQuant/Andromeda (version 2.6.5.0; Tyanova et al. 2016, Nature Protocols 11) and the UniProt human ProteomSet (including isoforms) for peptide and protein identification applying MaxQuant default settings. Relative protein quantification was performed using iBAQ values (Tyanova et al. 2016, Nature Protocols 11). MaxQuant iBAQ values of the different gel slices of a replicate were summed and the resulting intensities were normalized using variance-stabilization across the replicates of a bait protein or the control experiment (R package ‘vsn’, Huber et al. (2002), doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/18.suppl_1.S96). Missing values were imputed with the median within the corresponding protein group for the control experiment and by sampling from a normal distribution with 0.3 width and a single standard deviation downshift for the immunoisolated samples. Protein abundance ratios and p-values were calculated using rank sum analysis (R package ‘RankProd’; Del Carratore et al. (2017); 10.1093/bioinformatics/btx292). Data analysis was carried out using the autoprot package in Python (Bender et al (2024), doi: 10.1101/2024.01.18.571429).
