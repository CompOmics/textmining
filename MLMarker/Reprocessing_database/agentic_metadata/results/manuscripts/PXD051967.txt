ABSTRACT:
Posttranslational modifications of proteins (PTMs) greatly enhance their functional diversity, surpassing the number of gene-encoded variations. One intriguing PTM is ADP-ribosylation, which utilizes nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) as a substrate and is essential in cell signaling pathways regulating cellular responses. Here, we report the first cell-permeable NAD+ analogs that are modified with a dye or an affinity tag, respectively, and demonstrate their suitability as tools for investigating of the cellular ADP-ribosylation process. Utilizing desthiobiotin-tagged probes, we characterized ADP-ribosylome changes during oxidative stress in HeLa cells. We identified proteins previously described as ADP-ribosylation targets or closely associated with them. Therefore, we believe that our cell-permeable NAD+ probes offer reliable tools for comprehensive ADP-ribosylation investigations and understanding cellular responses to stress.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Identification of ADP-ribosylation targets in living HeLa cells using cell-permeable NAD+ probes

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
HeLa cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). The cells (30 000 cells/cm2 per plate) were seeded in 15 cm plates (Sarstedt) 24 h before the experiment. The cells were then washed with PBS and incubated with 10 µM Chol-2-NAD-DTB in Opti-MEM for 2 h at 37°C with 5% CO2. Subsequently, the cells were washed with PBS and oxidative stress was induced by treating them with a 1 mM H2O2 solution in PBS for 10 min at 37°C with 5% CO2. Control samples included cells incubated solely with NAD+ probe or without the NAD+ probe with and without H2O2 treatment. After several washing steps, 500 µL of cold lysis buffer (6 M guanidine hydrochloride, 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 2 mM Na orthovanadate, 2 mM NaF, and 5 mM glycerol-2-phosphate) was added to each plate, and the plates were incubated for 10 min on ice. The cells were then scraped and transferred to 2.0 mL low-binding tubes and sonicated using Bandelin Sonopuls HD 2200 ultrasonic homogenizer (3 x 15 pulses, 10% power). The supernatant was cleared by centrifugation at 14000 g and 4°C for 30 min, filled to 1.5 mL with lysis buffer without Gnd-HCl (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 2 mM Na orthovanadate, 2 mM NaF, and 5 mM glycerol-2-phosphate), and 1 mM DTT was added. To determine protein concentration, the Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was applied. Affinity enrichment was conducted using Pierce™ High Capacity Streptavidin Agarose (Thermo Fisher Scientific) beads. The beads were equilibrated with lysis buffer without Gnd-HCl, and 100 µL was added to each lysate sample. The suspensions were then incubated for 1.5 h at room temperature with mild shaking. Beads filtration, washing, and elution were carried out using Mobicol “F” columns with inserted 35 µm filters (MoBiTec), previously equilibrated with lysis buffer without Gnd-HCl. The beads-lysate mixtures were transferred to the columns and centrifuged for 90 s at 2700 rpm to obtain Flow-through (FT). The beads were then washed to remove non-specifically bound proteins through a series of consecutive steps: 3 x 100 µL of 1% SDS in PBS, 3 x 100 µL of washing buffer (4M Urea, 150 mM NaCl, 1% SDS in PBS), and 5 x 100 µL of 50 mM NH4HCO3. All washing solutions were placed on the top of beads, spun down (90 s, 2700 rpm), and collected as one Wash (W) fraction. Enriched proteins were then eluted from the beads by 3 x 15 min incubation with elution buffer (50 mM NH4HCO3, 0.8 mM biotin) at 37°C with 950 rpm shaking, combined with eluate centrifugation (90 s, 2700 rpm) into a fresh, low-binding tube. The three elution fraction were then combined into E (eluate) and lyophilized. Dry E samples were resuspended in 13 µL of PARG buffer (5 mM KH2PO4 pH 7.5, 5 mM KCl, 0.9 mM β-mercaptoethanol), and 1.5 µL of 100 nM of PARG enzyme (Sigma Aldrich) was added (final concentration 10.3 nM). The samples were then incubated for 4 h at 37°C. Afterwards the reactions were quenched with loading dye and incubation for 5 min at 95°C. Subsequently, samples were resolved slightly (around 1 cm long) on a 12.5% SDS-PAGE gel with 10 chambers, containing a 5% stacking gel layer. Proteins were then visualized using Colloidal Coomassie staining, cut out, divided into smaller gel pieces, and placed into 1.5 mL low-binding tubes. One sample was divided into three fractions: the main fraction, pockets, and the front of the sample (the fastest migrating fraction), and each was digested separately. Proteins were proteolyzed following the in-gel digestion protocol from Shevchenko et al.[1] with some modifications as published by Lehner et al.[2]. The experiment was conducted in four replicates.  References: [1] Shevchenko, A., Tomas, H., Havlis, J., Olsen, J. V., Mann, M., Nat Protoc 2006, 1, 2856-2860; [2] Lehner, M., Rieth, S., Höllmüller, E., Spliesgar, D., Mertes, B., Stengel, F., Marx, A., Angewandte Chemie International Edition, 2022, 61, e202200977.

DATA PROCESSING:
Affinity enrichment samples from each condition were analyzed in four biological replicates and additionally technical duplicates were measured. Raw files were analyzed with MaxQuant (version 2.1.4.0)[1] software with label-free quantification (minimum ratio count 2). To identify proteins the human reference proteome downloaded from UniProtKB database was used. Further data analysis was conducted using Perseus software (version 2.0.7.0).[2] Identified protein targets were filtered for only identified by site, reverse hits, and potential contaminants. LFQ intensity values were log2 transformed and filtered to be detected in at least 4 out of 8 replicates. The missing values were imputated from normal distribution (width: 0.3, down shift: 1.8). Significantly enriched proteins were identified using ANOVA multiple sample test (FDR = 0.05, s0 = 0.1), followed by Posthoc Turkey's honest significant (HSD) test for one-way ANOVA with grouping for biological replicates (FDR = 0.05). Enriched proteins were normalized by Z-scoring, analyzed by hierarchical clustering (Euclidean distance) and visualized in the form of heatmaps.  References: [1] Cox, J., Hein, M. Y., Luber, C. A., Paron, I., Nagaraj, N., Mann, M., Mol Cell Proteomics 2014, 13 (9), 2513-2526; Cox, J., Mann, M., Nat Biotechnol 2008, 26 (12), 1367-1372. [2] Tyanova, S., Temu, T., Sinitcyn, P., Carlson, A., Hein, M. Y., Geiger, T., Mann, M., Cox, J., Nat Methods 2016, 13 (9), 731-740.

ABSTRACT (PubMed):
Posttranslational modifications (PTMs) greatly enhance the functional diversity of proteins, surpassing the number of gene-encoded variations. One intriguing PTM is ADP-ribosylation, which utilizes nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD<sup>+</sup>) as a substrate and is essential in cell signaling pathways regulating cellular responses. Here, we report the first cell-permeable NAD<sup>+</sup> analogs and demonstrate their utility for investigating cellular ADP-ribosylation. Using a desthiobiotin-labelled analog for affinity enrichment of proteins that are ADP-ribosylated in living cells under oxidative stress, we identified protein targets associated with host-virus interactions, DNA damage and repair, protein biosynthesis, and ribosome biogenesis. Most of these targets have been noted in various literature sources, highlighting the potential of our probes for cellular ADP-ribosylome studies.
