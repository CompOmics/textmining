ABSTRACT:
This study characterised the array of benzylpenicillin-modified ligands within the immunopeptidome of HLA-A*02:01. We further describe a novel target of penicillin within the immunopeptidome and proteome. Identification of penicillin-modified ligands within the immunopeptidome will allow future immunogenicity assays to aid in understanding how T cells can mediate penicillin-induced hypersensitivities.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Naturally presented benzypenicillin-modified HLA-A*02:01 ligands

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
For immunopeptidomic analyses, peptides presented by HLA-A*02:01 on C1R.A*02:01 pellets were isolated and analysed via LC-MS/MS as described by Purcell et al. 2019. Cell pellets were lysed in a non-denaturing lysis buffer containing 0.5% IGEPAL-630 (Sigma-Aldrich), and HLA-A*02:01 were immunopurified by solid-phase bound anti-HLA-A2 antibody BB7.2. To isolate peptides from the other components of the HLA, samples were fractions by RP-HPLC. HPLC fractions containing peptides were pooled, vacuum concentrated, and spiked with 200 fmol of indexed retention time peptides prior to mass spectrometry analysis. To generate cysteinylated peptides containing BP adducts, peptides were first incubated with L-cysteine (Sigma Aldrich) at a molar ratio of 1:4 (Peptide:L-cysteine) for 3 hours at 37°C in PBS, followed by BP at a molar ratio of 1:2 (Peptide/L-cysteine:BP) for 24 hour at 37°C. Prior to MS analysis, peptides were desalted using OMIX C18 pipette tips (Agilent, CA, USA). Peptide MS/MS spectra were acquired using the same parameters used for the natural HLA peptide ligands.  For synthetic peptide analysis bearing a cysteine-benzylpenicillin modification, peptides were first incubated with L-cysteine (Sigma Aldrich) at a molar ratio of 1:4 (Peptide:L-cysteine) for 3 hours at 37°C in PBS. Cysteinylated peptides were then incubated with benzylpenicillin at a molar ratio of 1:2 (Peptide/L-cysteine:BP) for 24 hour at 37°C. Prior to MS analysis, peptides were desalted using OMIX C18 pipette tips.  For proteomic analyses, lysates of C1R.A*02:01 cells were denatured with urea and cysteines were reduced with TCEP in some condition. All samples were alkylated with iodoacetamide and digested with either trypsin or chymotrypsin. Prior to MS analysis, peptides were desalted using OMIX C18 pipette tips.

DATA PROCESSING:
MS/MS data were searched with PEAKS Xpro 10.6 (Bioinformatics Solutions Inc.) against the reviewed human proteome (UniProtKB/Swissprot, downloaded on 10/11/2018). All files went through default data refinement defined by PEAKS Xpro prior to a de novo-assisted database search.  For immunopeptidomics and synthetic peptide analyses, parent mass error tolerance of 20 ppm and fragment mass error tolerance of 0.02 Da were allowed. For all searches, enzyme specificity was turned off. A maximum of 3 variable modifications were allowed per peptide. The 5 variable modifications considered were oxidation of Met (+15.99), deamidation of Asn/Gln (+0.98), cysteinylation of Cys (+119.00), BP modification on cysteinylated Cys (CysBP) (+453.10), and BP modifications on Lys/Arg/His/Cys (+334.10). All analyses were conducted with a peptide false discovery rate (FDR) of 5%, which was estimated using a decoy fusion method.  For proteomic analyses, parent mass error tolerance of 20 ppm and fragment mass error tolerance of 0.02 Da were allowed. Enzyme specificity was set to either trypsin or chymotrypsin accordingly. A maximum of 3 variable modifications were allowed per peptide. The 5 variable modifications considered were oxidation of Met (+15.99), deamidation of Asn/Gln (+0.98), cysteinylation of Cys (+119.00), CysBP (+453.10), and BP modifications on Lys/Arg/His/Cys (+334.10). All analyses were conducted with a peptide FDRof 1%, which was estimated using a decoy fusion method.
