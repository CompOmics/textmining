ABSTRACT:
The alphaherpesvirus Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV) infects most humans. It causes chickenpox, shingles and rare severe central nervous system (CNS) pathologies. To gain molecular insights in the virus’ pathobiology, we conducted a proteomic survey on the interactions and effects of 64 VZV proteins, and VZV infection-induced perturbations, in neuronal SK-N-BE2 cells. This identified 900 interactors and 3618 regulated host proteins (https://varizonet.innatelab.org). Data integration and functional validations of the identified host proteins unveiled NPHP4 as VZV restriction factor. Combining whole exome of patients with VZV-associated CNS pathologies identified the NPHP4 S862N variant. Importantly, the S862N mutation impaired NPHP4's antiviral activity, and supressed its interactions with 14-3-3 proteins, as measured by affinity-purification.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Affinity-purification of HA-tagged NPHP4 wild-type and S862N mutant in SK-N-BE2 cells

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
SK-N-BE2 cells transduced with NPHP4 wild-type, S862M mutant or GFP fused to N-terminal HA-tag were expended in quadruplicates to reach two confluent 15cm dishes per replicate. Cells were gently washed in ice-cold PBS, scraped off, pooled per replicate and washed two times in ice-cold PBS by centrifugation at 600g at 10°C for 10 minutes. All samples were flashed frozen in liquid nitrogen before storage at -80°C. Frozen cell pellets were thawed and lysed on ice for 30 minutes in 1ml lysis buffer (0.2% NP-40, 100mM NaCl, 5% glycine, 1.5mM MgCl2, 50mM Tris/HCl pH 7.5) supplemented with 1% in-house benzonase and EDTA-free Complete protease inhibitor (Roche). Samples were sonicated (5 minutes, 4°C, 30 seconds on and off, high frequency; Bioruptor, Diagenode SA) and centrifuged at 15000g at 4°C for 30 minutes. Total protein concentrations were measured by Pierce 660nm Protein Assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and normalised to 2mg in 1ml lysis buffer supplemented with EDTA-Free Complete protease inhibitors (Roche). Cleared lysates were mixed with 40ul of anti-HA agarose bead slurry (Sigma Aldrich, A2095), previously equilibrated in lysis buffer, and agitated for three hours at 4°C. Immuno-complexes attached to the beads were washed five times with 1ml lysis buffer and four time in 1ml wash buffer (lysis buffer without NP-40) to eliminate remaining detergents. Excess buffer was removed and beads were resuspended in 20ul of 1:10 diluted Guanidinium Chloride buffer (0.6M GdmCl, 1mM TCEP, 4mM CAA in 0.1M Tris/HCl pH 8.0) to allow denaturation, reduction and alkylation of the enriched proteins, and frozen at -20°C. Sample were thawed at room temperature and pre-digested with 0.5µg LysC (Wako Chemicals) for 3 hours at 37°C, followed by a 1:4 dilution in 0.1M Tris/HCl pH 8.0 and digested for additional 16 hours at 30°C with 0.5µg trypsin (Promega, Sequencing Grade). Digest was stopped and peptides solubilised in 0.6% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) and 2% acetonitrile (ACN). Beads were sedimented by spin for 5 minutes at 10000rpm, peptides were transferred into a new tubes, processed by StageTip purification with three layers of C18 Empore filter discs (3M) and resuspended in 2% acetonitrile, 0.1% TFA. Mass spectrometry analysis was performed on an EASY-nLC 1200 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific), coupled online with the mass spectrometer (Q Exactive HF-X, Thermo Fisher Scientific) via a nano-electrospray source as described before (Stukaloc et al., Nature, 2021). Briefly, peptides were eluted on a 20cm reverse-phase analytical column (75 µm diameter; ReproSil-Pur C18-AQ 1.9 µm resin; Dr. Maisch) by a gradient of ACN in 0.1% formic acid at a flow rate of 300nl per minute (sequential linear gradients of 80% ACN: 5-30% for 37 minutes, 30-60% for 6 minutes, 60-80% for 3 minutes, 80-95% for 1 minutes, followed by a stationary step at 95% for 5 minutes to elute the most hydrophobic peptides, and re-equilibration of the column at 5%). The column was washed with 95% of 80% ACN for 15 minutes in between each quadruplicates. The mass spectrometer was operated and MS spectra acquired using the XCalibur software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Full MS scan (300-1650 m/z, R = 60 000) were acquired at an ion target of 3e6. The top 15 most abundant precursor peptides fragmented by higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with a normalized collision energy of 27% and MS/MS scan (R = 15 000) acquired at an ion target of 1e5 and a maximum injection time of 25ms. Isolation and fragmentation of the same peptide precursor was eliminated by dynamic exclusion for 20s.

DATA PROCESSING:
The raw MS data files were analyzed with MaxQuant (version 1.6.14.0) using the standard settings, match between run and label-free quantification (LFQ) enabled (LFQ min ratio count 2, stabilize large LFQ ratios disabled and normalization skipped). The MS2 spectra were searched against forward and reverse (used as decoy) sequences of the reviewed human proteome including isoforms (UniprotKB, release 2019) and the GFP by the built-in Andromeda search engine.
