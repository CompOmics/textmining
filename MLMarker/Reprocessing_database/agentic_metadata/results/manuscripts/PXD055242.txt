ABSTRACT:
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is India's second largest cause of death and is largely caused by smoking. Asymptomatic smokers develop COPD due to genetic, environmental, and molecular variables, making early screening crucial. Data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry (DIA-MS) proteomics offers an unbiased method to analyze proteomic profiles. This study is the first to use DIA-based proteomics to analyze individual serum samples from three distinct male cohorts: healthy individuals (n=10), asymptomatic smokers (n=10), and COPD patients (n=10). This comprehensive approach identified 667 proteins with a 1% false discovery rate. Differentially expressed proteins included 40 in the normal versus asymptomatic comparison, 88 in the COPD versus normal comparison, and 40 in the COPD versus asymptomatic comparison. Among them, PRDX6, ELANE, PRKCSH, PRTN3, and MNDA could help differentiate COPD from asymptomatic smokers, while ELANE, H3-3A, IGHE, SLC4A1, and SERPINA11 could differentiate COPD from healthy subjects. Pathway enrichment and protein-protein interaction analyses revealed significant alterations in hemostasis, immune system functions, fibrin clot formation, and post-translational protein modifications. Key proteins were validated using a parallel reaction monitoring assay. DIA data are available via ProteomeXchange with identifier PXD055242. Our findings reveal key protein classifiers in COPD patients, asymptomatic smokers, and healthy individuals, helping clinicians understand disease pathobiology and improve disease management and quality of life.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Meta-Analysis and DIA-MS-based Proteomic Investigation of COPD Patients and Asymptomatic Smokers in the Indian population

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Approximately 15 µL of serum was applied to Pierce Top 14 Abundant Protein Depletion Spin Columns (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and incubated on a rotating shaker for 1 hour. After incubation, the serum samples were eluted by centrifugation at 1500 g for 2 minutes at room temperature. The eluted serum was then concentrated to one-fourth of its original volume using a SpeedVac. The Bradford assay was conducted on the depleted serum samples, diluted at a 1:20 ratio, to measure protein concentration. To visualize differential protein patterns, Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate–Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) was performed. For in-solution digestion, 30 µg of protein from the depleted serum was used. The volume was first brought to 20 µL with 6 M urea, followed by the addition of 20 mM tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine (TCEP) and incubation at 37 °C for 1 hour. Next, 40 mM IAA was added, and the mixture was incubated in the dark at room temperature for 15 minutes. The pH was adjusted to 8 using 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate (ABC), adding 60 µL incrementally until the desired pH was achieved. Trypsin digestion was carried out by adding 4 µL of trypsin (0.25 µg/µL) at a 1:30 ratio and incubating overnight at 37°C. The following day, the mixture was fully dried in a SpeedVac. The dried peptides were reconstituted in 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) and cleaned using SepPak® C18 cartridges. This cleaning process involved conditioning with 10:90 MS water , equilibration with 0.1% TFA, washing with 0.1% TFA, desalting with 5:95 MS water , and eluting with 50% acetonitrile (ACN). The cleaned samples were then dried completely in a SpeedVac and reconstituted in 15 µL of 2% ACN and 0.1% formic acid (FA). Peptide quantification was performed at 205 and 280 nm before the samples were submitted for mass spectrometry analysis.

DATA PROCESSING:
The raw data files from the Orbitrap Fusion Tribrid Mass Spectrometer were processed using DIA-NN (version 1.8.1) against the UniProt Human database, which contains around 20,000 protein-coding genes. For protein identification, the false discovery rate (FDR) was set to 1%, with a minimum peptide length requirement of 7. The resulting .pg_matrix file from DIA-NN was used for further analysis. Features with over 30% missing values were excluded, and additional imputation was performed using the k-nearest neighbor (KNN) method (feature-wise), followed by median normalization. The normalized data were then analyzed to explore group-wise clustering through principal component analysis (PCA). Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were identified with a p-value of <0.05 and a fold change (FC) of 1.5. The data analysis and visualization were conducted using Metaboanalyst 5.0, Microsoft Excel 2016, and Python. Altered gene expression was illustrated using balloon plots generated by SRPlot. DEPs from inter- and intra-group comparisons were mapped to biological pathways using the Reactome database. The proteins or protein-associated genes from these enriched pathways were compared with existing literature to gain insights into their biological roles. Additionally, the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network for gene ontology (GO) pathway enrichment analysis was visualized using STRING. The network was set to display physical sub-networks, with edges representing confidence levels and a minimum interaction score of 0.7 (high confidence), with no clustering applied.
