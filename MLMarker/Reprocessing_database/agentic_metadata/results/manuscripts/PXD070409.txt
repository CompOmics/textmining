ABSTRACT:
This dataset contains proteomic data from co-immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry (IP-MS) experiments performed to identify in planta interaction partners of the transcriptional regulator SPOROCYTELESS/NOZZLE (SPL) during Arabidopsis thaliana ovule development. SPL is an essential regulator of megaspore mother cell (MMC) specification, yet the protein complexes through which it acts remained largely unknown. To elucidate the SPL interactome, a pSPL:SPL-GFP fusion was expressed in the pAP1:AP1-GR ap1 cal floral inducible system, allowing synchronized flower development and enrichment of ovule stages preceding MMC formation. SPL-GFP protein complexes were isolated from inflorescences at early ovule developmental stages and analysed by LC-MS/MS. The IP-MS analysis revealed 660 proteins significantly enriched in SPL-GFP immunoprecipitates compared with input controls, providing the first comprehensive view of SPL-associated nuclear partners in reproductive tissues. Among transcription factors co-purifying with SPL, MADS-domain proteins were particularly prominent, representing about one fifth of all enriched TFs. These included SEEDSTICK (STK), SHATTERPROOF2 (SHP2), SEPALLATA1-3 (SEP1–3), and AGAMOUS (AG)—core components of the ovule identity regulatory network. The detection of these ovule-specific MADS-domain transcription factors among SPL interactors strongly supports a model in which SPL is recruited into multimeric MADS-domain complexes that coordinate nucellus development and MMC specification.  The SPL IP-MS dataset therefore provides crucial insight into the protein interaction landscape controlling female germline initiation in Arabidopsis. It identifies SPL as a central component of ovule identity complexes and reveals a biochemical connection between SPL activity and the MADS-mediated transcriptional network that defines nucellar fate and auxin-dependent MMC differentiation.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Plants were grown under long day conditions on soil. Induced primary cauliflower-like inflorescences (0.75 g) were ground in liquid nitrogen. The tissue powder was resuspended in 20 ml M1 buffer (10 mM sodium phosphate buffer pH 7.0, 0.1 M NaCl, 1 M 2-methyl 2.4-pentanediol, 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1x cOmplete protease inhibitor cocktail) and filtered through a 55-µm nylon mesh. The filtrate was centrifuged at 1,000g for 20 min at 4 °C. The pellet was washed by complete resuspension 5x with 5 ml M2 buffer (M1 buffer with 10 mM MgCl2, 0.5 % Triton X-100) and 1x with with 5 ml M3 buffer (M1 buffer without 2-methyl 2,4-pentanediol), centrifuged at 1,000g for 10 min at 4 °C in between. The resulting pellet was resuspended in 1 ml Lysis Buffer (μMACS GFP Isolation Kit; Miltenyi Biotec) supplemented with 1x cOmplete protease inhibitor cocktail and ~0.125 U/µl Benzonase nuclease (Novagen). The sample was sonicated using Covaris S220 with the following settings: Peak Power = 140.0; Cycles/Burst = 200; Duty Factor = 5.0; Duration = 360 sec; temp = 6°C. The protein lysate was cleared by 2x centrifugation at 20,000g for 10 min at 4 °C. 25 µl of the supernatant was set aside for the input sample preparation and 10 µl for the total protein amount estimation. 50 µl anti-GFP beads (μMACS GFP Isolation Kit; Miltenyi Biotec) were added to the rest of the supernatant and incubated on a rotator at 10 rpm for 1 h at 4 °C. The mix was applied to the magnetic column and column-immobilized beads were washed 5 times with 200 µl Lysis Buffer and 2 times with 200 µl Wash Buffer 2 (μMACS GFP Isolation Kit; Miltenyi Biotec). 20 µl of 8 M urea was applied to the column and incubated 5 min at room temperature (RT). Immunoprecipitated proteins (IP sample) were eluted with 50 µl 8 M urea. Proteins were: reduced with 5 mM DTT for 30 min at 56 °C with 700 rpm and cooled down to RT; alkylated with 10 mM iodoacetamide for 30 min at RT in the dark and diluted to the final urea concentration at 1.6 M with 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0 (digestion buffer); digested with 0.5 µg of trypsin/LysC (Trypsin/Lys-C Mix, Mass Spec Grade, Promega) at 37°C overnight (O/N). Peptides were acidified with 1% formic acid (FA) and desalted using Oasis HLB 96-well µElution Plate (Waters) using two 300 µl washes with 1% FA and eluted twice with 50 µl 50% acetonitrile (ACN)/0.5% FA solution. Peptides were dried down using a SpeedVac for 1.5 h at 60 °C with 5.1 mTor and stored at -20 °C. Total protein amount in the supernatant was estimated using Micro BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo). Input sample was processed with the modified FASP method. 200 µl of 8 M urea in 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0 (UT buffer) was added to 25 µg of protein. Proteins were reduced with 10 mM DTT for 30 min at 56 °C with 700 rpm, cooled down to RT and alkylated with 20 mM iodoacetamide for 30 min at RT in the dark. Protein solution was transferred to the Amicon Ultra-0.5 Centrifugal Filter 30 kDa MWCO (Millipore) and centrifuged at 14,000g for 10 min at RT. The sample was washed 3x with 200 µl UT buffer and 3x with digestion buffer each time centrifuging in between. The filter unit was placed in a new collection tube and 100 µl digestion buffer and 0.5 µg of of trypsin/LysC (Trypsin/Lys-C Mix, Mass Spec Grade, Promega) was added and incubated in a wet chamber at 37 °C O/N. Input sample was eluted from the filter by centrifugation and two washes with 100 µl digestion buffer each time followed by centrifugation. The peptide-containing filtrate was acidified with 1% FA and desalted in the same way as the IP sample.  Dried peptides were resuspended in 25 µl 0.1% FA/2% ACN, vortexed and sonicated in a water-bath sonicator for 10 min at RT. Peptide concentration was estimated using NanoDrop One A205 method. Samples were analyzed using the EASY-nLC 1200 (Thermo) coupled to the Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo). Approximately 1-2 µg of peptides were loaded onto the Acclaim PepMap 100 C18 (75 μm inner diameter, 15 cm long, 2 µm particle size, Thermo) analytical column with 5% (v/v) solvent B (80% ACN/0.1% FA) in solvent A (0.1% FA) and separated with a linear solvent B gradient of 5% to 40 % over 70 min and 40% to 50% over 5 min followed by a washing step with 100% solvent B for 10 min and an equilibration step with 5% solvent B for 5 min at a flow rate of 300 nl/min. Full MS spectra were acquired with a scan range of 300-1650 m/z, resolution of 70,000, AGC target of 3e6 and maximum IT of 50 ms. Tandem MS spectra (MS/MS) were acquired in the data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode using a TOP15 method with a scan range of 200-2000 m/z, resolution of 17,500, AGC target of 1e5, maximum IT of 50 ms, isolation window of 2.5 m/z and normalized collision energy at 25%. Only peptide reaching intensity threshold of 3.4e4, with +2, +3, +4, +5, +6 charge state were selected for MS/MS fragmentation. Dynamic exclusion was set to 30 s.

DATA PROCESSING:
The RAW peptide sequencing data obtained from the Q Exactive Plus were processed by the MaxQuant (version 2.1.0.0) software. Settings of the MaxQuant were mostly kept default with “label-free quantification (LFQ)”, “iBAQ” and “Match between runs” options on. The protein database was Arabidopsis thaliana UniProt UP000006548 without protein isoforms. After the MaxQuant analysis, the output proteinGroups.txt file was taken for further filtering and statistical analysis in the Perseus (version 1.6.15.0) software. The protein LFQ intensity data was log2-transformed. Proteins identified by only modified peptides (“Only identified by site”), by reverse sequence database (“Reverse”) and protein contaminants (“Potential Contaminant”) were removed from the analysis. Proteins with LFQ intensity values in at least two biological replicates and proteins with at least two unique peptides were kept. Missing protein LFQ intensity values were replaced only in the input samples by the imputation from the left arm of the LFQ intensity normal distribution (0.3 width, 1.8 down shift, separately for each column) to emulate background protein level. Relative protein amount differences between different conditions were evaluated by two-sample t-tests with FDR correction.
