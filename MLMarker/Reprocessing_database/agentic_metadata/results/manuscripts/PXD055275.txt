ABSTRACT:
Comprenhensive proteomic analysis of HCEC using Trapped Ion Mobility Spectrometry Time-of-Flight Pro2, Orbitrap Fusion Lumos and ZenoTrap Time-of-Flight 7600 Mass Spectrometer

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Data for Cross-platform Construction of Comprehensive Proteomic Database for Human Corneal Epithelial Cell

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Immortalized Human Corneal Epithelial Cell (HCECs #BNCC337876, BeNa Culture Collection, China) were cultured with Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium F12 (DMEM/F12; #11320033, ThermoFisher, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; #A5256701, ThermoFisher, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin antibiotic mixture (P/S; #15140122, ThermoFisher, USA) at 37 °C with 95% humidity and 5% CO2. Culture medium was renewed every 48 hours. HCECs between passages 4 to 8 were used in this study. Before harvesting for subsequent analysis, HCECs were cultured in FBS-free DMEM/F12 with 1%P/S and then incubated at 37 °C with 95% humidity and 5% CO2 for 24 hours prior to protein extraction. 

HCECs were washed with Ice-cold 1X Phosphate buffered saline (PBS; 137 mM NaCl, 2.7 mM KCl, 10 mM Na2HPO4, 1.8 mM KH2PO4, pH 7.4) six times. EasyPep Lysis buffer was then added to lyse the HCECs. The concentration of the proteins extracted was further adjusted for subsequent sample digestion and purification using EasyPep MS sample preparation kit with the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. In brief, proteins were reduced and alkylated with dithiothreitol (DTT) and iodoacetamide (IAA). Proteins were then digested with Trypsin and Lys-C. Digested peptides were cleaned up with the aid of reverse phase C18 column. Purified peptides were dried and then reconstituted with 0.1% formic acid (FA). Peptides were quantified and diluted to the concentration of 0.25 µg/µL prior to mass spectrometric analysis. 

One microgram of the samples was analyzed by LC-coupled Orbitrap Fusion Lumos Mass Spectrometer (Orbitrap; ThermoFisher, USA), Nano LC-coupled Trapped Ion Mobility Spectrometry Time-of-Flight Pro2 Mass Spectrometer (TimsTOF; Bruker, USA) and Micro LC-coupled ZenoTrap Time-of-Flight 7600 Mass Spectrometer (ZenoTOF; SCIEX, USA) in data-independent acquisition (DIA) respectively. For the sample fractionation in all three LC-MS, solvent A (0.1% FA in H2O) and solvent B (0.1% FA in acetonitrile) were used as the gradient.  

For Orbitrap, peptides were loaded onto the Acclaim™ PepMap™ 100 HPLC Column (100 Å, 5 μm, 5 mm x 1 mm, C18, Thermo Scientific, USA) by loading buffer (0.1% formic acid in water) at 0.3 μL/min for 2 min, and then separated on Aurora Ultimate nanoflow UHPLC column (120 Å, 1.7 µm, 25 cm x 75 µm; Ionopticks, Australia) using Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano NanoLC system (Thermo Scientific, USA) connect to the Orbitrap Fusion. The peptides were then fractionated by a 90-minute linear elution gradient (0-2min: 2-6%B, 2-79min: 6-30% B, 79-82min: 30-90%B, 82-87min: 90%B, 87-90min: 2%B). For the High-Resolution MS1 Data-Independent Acquisition (HRMS1-DIA) experiment, the MS1 scan range was set at 400-1500m/z in positive mode with a maximal injection time of 50 milliseconds, orbitrap resolution at 30,000 and standard AGC target. For the data independent analysis, the scan range was set 200-2000 m/z with orbitrap resolution at 7,500 and. HCD collision energy and RF lens were set at 30%. Ten 15 m/z isolation windows and twenty-five 10 m/z isolation windows were assigned within the mass range of 400-650 m/z and 650-800 m/z respectively with 1 m/z of window overlap in quadrupole mode.  

For TimsTOF, peptides were loaded onto the Acclaim™ PepMap™ 100 HPLC Column by loading buffer (0.1% f FA in H2O) at 0.3 μL/min for 2 min, and then separated on an Aurora Ultimate CSI Column (25 cm x 75 µm, 1.7 µm, C18, Ionopticks, AU) using Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano NanoLC system connected to the TimsTOF Pro2. The peptides were then fractionated by a 45-minute linear elution gradient (0-2min: 2%B, 2-2.1min: 2-5%B, 2.1-33min: 5-35%B, 33-33.5min: 35-90%B, 33.5-37min: 90%B, 37-37.1min: 90-2%B, 37.1-45min: 2%B). For Data-Independent Acquisition with Parallel Accumulation Serial Fragmentation (DIA-PASEF) experiments, the range of MS1 scan was 100-1700 m/z in positive mode. While for the data-independent analysis, twenty-one 25 m/z isolation windows were assigned over the mass range of 475-1000 m/z with 100% duty cycle, 100 ms accumulation and ramp time in the TIMS. 

For ZenoTOF, peptides were loaded on the nanoEase M/Z Symmetry trap column (100A, 5μm, 300μm x 50mm, C18, Waters, USA) by loading buffer (0.1% FA in H2O) at 10μL/min for 3min, then separated on a nanoEase M/Z HSS T3 Column (1.8μm, 75μm x 200mm, C18, Waters, USA) using an MicroLC system (UPLC M-Class, Waters) connected to ZenoTOF. The peptides were then fractionated by a 15-minute linear elution gradient (0-1min: 3%B, 1-9min: 10%B, 9-19min, 30%B, 10-10.2min, 40%B, 10.2-12.1min, 95%B, 12.1-15min, 3%B) were used. For DIA-SWATH experiments, isolation width from 3-100 Da is set in a looped mode over the full mass range (100-1500m/z) scan and 64 overlapping windows are constructed, with 20ms accumulation time per cycle using Zeno-pulsing. 

DATA PROCESSING:
The raw data files generated from ZenoTOF, Orbitrap and TimsTOF were imported to Spectronaut 19 (V.19; Biognosys, Switzerland) and DIA-NN (V 1.9.1, Cambridge, UK) for identifying and quantifying the relative abundances of the proteins in the samples. For Spectronaut, proteomic analysis was conducted in library-free DIA-directDIA mode. Reviewed Homo sapiens protein sequence database (FASTA) from UniProt and gene ontology annotation (GOA) of Homo sapiens from Gene Ontology (retrieved on 27-Dec-2023) were imported for proteomic search and functional annotation. The FASTA file consisted of 20,428 protein sequences including isoform and the GOA composed of 19,653 entries. Proteomic identification and quantification were run with the BGS factory default settings, and cleavage rules of Trypsin/P and LysC/P were applied. For DIA-NN, library-free search and library generation were conducted with the same database (FASTA) mentioned above, with deep learning-based spectra, RTs, and IMs prediction enabled and cleavage rule of Trypsin/P applied. All proteomic searches were conducted with 1% FDR. The resultant proteomic profiles that were generated from DIA-NN and Spectronaut were integrated based on protein group identity. The consistency of the protein groups was assessed by calculating the percentage of successful identifications relative to the total number of biological and technical replicates performed.
