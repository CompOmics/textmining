ABSTRACT:
Immunoprecipitation of Ub-GFP and Ub-GFP-tail expressed in HCT116 cells using GFP-Trap.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Interactome of Ub-GFP and Ub-GFP-tail

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Parental HCT116 cells and HCT116 cells expressing Ub-GFP or Ub-GFP-tail were lysed in buffer A (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5, 100 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 10 mM iodoacetamide (Sigma-Aldrich)) containing 0.2% NP-40, 10 μM bortezomib, 10 μM PR619 (LifeSensors) and complete protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche, EDTA-free). After incubation on ice for 30 min, the lysate was sonicated using a Handy Sonic (ACTIVE MOTIF) and then clarified by centrifugation at 20,000×g for 15 min at 4°C. Protein concentration was determined using the BCA protein assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). For anti-GFP immunoprecipitation, 1 mg of cell lysate was incubated with GFP-Trap Agarose beads (Proteintech) for 1 h at 4°C. After washing three times with either buffer A containing 0.2% NP-40, proteins were then eluted by incubating the beads for 5 min at 95°C in 1× NuPAGE LDS sample buffer. Protein samples were subjected to trypsin digestion using the SP3 protocol on a KingFisher APEX system. Specifically, for SP3-based digestion, magnetic carboxylate-modified beads (Cytiva Sera-Mag SpeedBead Carboxylate-Modified Magnetic Particles, hydrophilic and hydrophobic, mixed 1:1) were used. Proteins were first reduced and alkylated using 10 mM DTT (10 min, 70 °C) and 15 mM IAA (15 min, room temperature, dark), followed by binding to beads in 50% ethanol. The KingFisher APEX was programmed to perform sequential washing (3× with 80% ethanol) and digestion (37°C, 3.5 h) in 100 μL of TEAB buffer containing 0.5 μg Trypsin Gold (Mass Spectrometry Grade, dissolved in 50 mM triethylammonium bicarbonate (TEAB, Thermo Fisher Scientific)). After digestion, peptides were eluted with 100 μL MS-grade water and acidified with 0.1% TFA. Samples were then centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatants were collected. Desalting was performed using GL-Tip SDB, involving equilibration with buffer B (80% acetonitrile, 0.1% TFA), sample loading, and washing with buffer A (0.1% TFA). Samples were then analyzed by connecting an Easy-nLC 1200 to an Orbitrap Fusion Lumos Tribrid mass spectrometerequipped with a Nanospray Flex Ion Source. Peptides were separated on a C18 analytical column (3 µm particle size, 75 µm diameter × 120 mm length, Nikkyo Technos #NTCC-360/75-3-125), using a 75-min gradient of acetonitrile at a flow rate of 300 nL/min (0–60 min: acetonitrile from 0% to 0%, 60–70 min: acetonitrile from 40% to 80%, and 70–75 min: maintained at 80%). Data were acquired using a data-dependent acquisition (DDA) method with a mass range of 350–2,000 m/z and a resolution of 500,000 using the Xcalibur software 2.2. The HCD was set to 30 with a 3-second cycle selecting the most intense ions.

DATA PROCESSING:
MS raw files were analyzed using Proteome Discoverer 3.2 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with the Sequest HT search engine against the Homo sapiens reference proteome (UniProt, version 2024-10-02) appended with an EGFP sequence. The precursor and fragment mass tolerances were set to 10 ppm and 0.02 Da, respectively. Methionine oxidation, protein amino-terminal acetylation, diglycine-lysine, and glutamine/asparagine deamidation were set as variable modifications, and cysteine carbamidomethylation was set as a static modification for database searching. Maximum missed cleavage sites of trypsin were set to 2. Peptide identification was filtered at false discovery rate < 0.01. Label-free, intensity-based protein quantification was performed using the Precursor Ions Quantifier node in Proteome Discoverer 3.2.
