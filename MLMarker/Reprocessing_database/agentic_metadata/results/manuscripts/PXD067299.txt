ABSTRACT:
Liquid biopsy is increasingly recognized for tumor diagnosis and monitoring, and in medulloblastoma (MB) and other CNS tumors, it offers the potential to bypass invasive tissue collection. However, its application in clinical practice remains limited, in part because the optimal choice of biofluid is underexplored and may differ between tumor types. Here, we report mass spectrometry–based proteomic analysis of extracellular vesicles (EVs) from three matched cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and blood plasma (BP) samples of MB patients, alongside healthy BP controls and MB cell lines.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Medulloblastoma LC-MS/MS using small extracellular vesicles from blood plasma, CSF, and MB cell lines

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
sEV preparation sEVs were isolated using ultrafiltration combined with size exclusion chromatography (blood plasma, CSF) or by combining tangential flow filtration, size exclusion chromatography, and ultrafiltration (cell lines).  Protein reduction, alkylation, digestion, and peptide labeling Proteins were reduced by incubating with 10 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) in 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 8.5) at 56°C for 30 minutes to break disulfide bonds. Subsequently, free cysteines were alkylated using 20 mM 2-chloroacetamide in the same buffer at room temperature for 30 minutes in the dark. Protein cleanup and digestion were performed following the Single-Pot Solid-Phase-enhanced Sample Preparation (SP3) protocol (DOI: 10.15252/msb.20145625; 10.1038/s41596-018-0082-x). Trypsin (sequencing grade, Promega) was added at a 1:50 enzyme-to-protein ratio, and samples were digested overnight at 37°C in 50 mM HEPES buffer. Tryptic peptides were labeled using TMT16plex isobaric tagging reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific), following the manufacturer’s instructions (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.9b04474). Briefly, 0.8 mg of the TMT reagent was dissolved in 42 µL of 100% acetonitrile. From this solution, 4 µL was added to each peptide sample and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. Labelling reactions were quenched by adding 4 µL of 5% hydroxylamine and incubating for 15 minutes at room temperature. Samples within each experimental set were pooled and desalted using an OASIS® HLB µElution Plate (Waters). LC-MS/MS Analysis Peptides were analyzed on an UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to an Orbitrap Fusion™ Lumos™ Tribrid™ mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). For sample loading, a trapping cartridge (µ-Precolumn C18 PepMap™ 100, 300 µm ID × 5 mm, 5 µm particle size, 100 Å pore size) was used, followed by separation on an analytical column (nanoEase™ M/Z HSS T3, 75 µm ID × 250 mm, 1.8 µm particle size, 100 Å pore size; Waters). Samples were trapped at 30 µL/min using 0.05% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) in water for 6 minutes. Peptides were then separated using a linear gradient of increasing solvent B (3% DMSO, 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile) over solvent A (3% DMSO, 0.1% formic acid in water), at a flow rate of 0.3 µL/min. Mass Spectrometry Settings Peptides were ionized using a Pico-Tip emitter (360 µm OD × 20 µm ID, 10 µm tip, CoAnn Technologies) with an applied spray voltage of 2.2 kV. The capillary temperature was maintained at 275 °C. Full MS (MS1) scans were acquired in profile mode across an m/z range of 375–1650, at a resolution of 120,000 (at m/z 200) in the Orbitrap analyzer. The AGC (automatic gain control) target was set to 'standard', and the maximum injection time was 50 ms. The instrument was operated in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. MS/MS (MS2) scans were acquired in the Orbitrap at a resolution of 30,000, with a maximum injection time of 110 ms and an AGC target of 200%. Precursor ions were isolated using a quadrupole isolation window of 0.7 m/z and fragmented using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with a normalized collision energy (NCE) of 34%. Dynamic exclusion was enabled for 20 seconds. Only precursor ions with charge states from +2 to +7 were selected for fragmentation. MS2 spectra were recorded in profile mode. Database searching and quantification Raw data were processed using IsobarQuant (DOI: 10.1038/nprot.2015.101) in conjunction with Mascot (v2.2.07). Database searches were performed against the UniProt Homo sapiens reference proteome (UP000005640, May 2016 release, 92,507 entries), supplemented with common contaminants and reversed decoy sequences. Search parameters included: fixed modifications (carbamidomethylation of cysteine, TMT16plex on lysine), variable modifications (TMT16plex on peptide N-termini, oxidation of methionine, N-terminal protein acetylation). Trypsin was specified as the proteolytic enzyme, allowing up to two missed cleavages. Mass tolerances (precursor ions: 10 ppm, fragment ions: 0.02 Da), minimum peptide length: 7 amino acids, protein quantification criteria: at least two unique peptides, false discovery rate (FDR): 1% at both peptide and protein levels.

DATA PROCESSING:
Proteomics Data Analysis Raw output files generated by IsobarQuant (specifically, protein.txt files) were processed using the R programming environment (ISBN: 3-900051-07-0). The initial step involved the removal of common contaminants and reverse (decoy) protein entries. Only proteins quantified with at least two unique peptides and a qupm (quantified peptides per protein) ≥ 2 were retained for downstream analysis. A total of 210 proteins passed these quality control filters.  To address potential technical variability, batch effects were corrected using the removeBatchEffect function from the limma package (DOI: 10.1093/nar/gkv007), applied to the log₂-transformed TMT reporter ion intensity values (extracted from the signal_sum columns). Normalization of the data was then performed using the normalizeVSN function, implementing variance stabilization normalization (VSN) (DOI: 10.1093/bioinformatics/18.suppl_1.s96). Differential expression analysis was conducted using the moderated t-test provided by the limma package. Replicate structure was incorporated into the analysis via the design matrix used in the lmFit function. Proteins were categorized as follows: - Hits: FDR < 0.05 and absolute fold change > 2 - Candidates: FDR < 0.2 and absolute fold change > 1.5 To explore patterns in protein abundance, clustering analysis was performed on both hit and candidate proteins. Protein intensities were first normalized by the median of the control condition. Clustering was based on median abundances using the k-means method and Euclidean distance as the distance metric. Hierarchical clustering was performed using Ward's D2 linkage. The optimal number of clusters (k = 10) was determined using the Elbow method, which identifies the point at which the within-cluster sum of squares begins to level off. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted using the compareCluster function from the clusterProfiler package (PMID: 22455463). The analysis was performed across the three major GO categories: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular compartment (CC).  Annotations were based on the org.Hs.eg.db reference database. Enrichment significance was assessed using the odds ratio, calculated by comparing the GeneRatio (the proportion of genes associated with a specific GO term in the dataset) to the BgRatio (the same proportion in the background gene set). An odds ratio > 1 indicates an overrepresentation of the corresponding GO term in the input dataset.
