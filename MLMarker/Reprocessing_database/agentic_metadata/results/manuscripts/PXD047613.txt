ABSTRACT:
ADP-ribosylation (ADPr) signaling plays a crucial role in the DNA damage response. Inhibitors against the main enzyme catalyzing ADPr after DNA damage – PARP1 – are used as targeted therapies against breast cancers with BRCA1/2 mutations. However, development of resistance to PARP inhibitors (PARPi) is a major obstacle in treating patients. To better understand the role of ADPr in PARPi sensitivity, we used Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) for systems level analysis of the ADP-ribosylome in six breast cancer cell lines with different PARPi sensitivities. We identified 1632 sites on 777 proteins, primarily on serine residues, with a high site overlap of DNA damage-related proteins across all cell lines, demonstrating high conservation in ADPr signaling after DNA damage. We furthermore observed site-specific differences in ADPr intensities in PARPi-sensitive BRCA mutants, and unique ADPr sites in PARPi-resistant BRCA mutant cells, which we notably show to have low PARG levels and longer PARP1 ADPr chains.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
For proteome MS, cells were lysed with guanidinium-HCl (GndHCl) buffer (6M guanidine-HCl, 50 mM Tris pH 8.5). For proteome MS, breast cancer cells were grown to 80% confluency, washed twice with cold PBS, harvested on ice with a cell scraper, and pelleted. Cells were lysed with 2mL GndHCl lysis buffer (6M guanidine-HCl, 50 mM Tris pH 8.5). 5 mM TCEP and 10 mM CAA were added to lysates for reduction of disulfide bonds and methionine alkylation. Lysates were sonicated on ice for 45s at 60% amplitude. After 1h incubation at RT, proteins were digested with 1:200 w/w Lys-C endopeptidase for 3h, followed by overnight digestion with Trypsin (1:200 w/w) after diluting to 1.5M GndHCl with 25 mM Tris pH 8.5. Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) was added to 0.5% v/v and samples were centrifuged at high speed to pellet precipitates. Peptides were purified using reverse-phase Sep-Pak C18 cartridges according to manufacturer’s instructions. eluted in 30% Acetonitrile (ACN) in 0.1% TFA, and vacuum-dried in a SpeedVac at 60°C. Peptides were reconstituted in 50 mM Ammonium bicarbonate (ABC) and separated on an Acuity UPLC Peptide CSH C18 1.7 µm reversed-phase column under basic conditions, concatenation into 12 fractions (Batth and Olsen, 2016). Formic acid (FA) was added to peptides at 0.5% v/v, followed by vacuum-drying at 60°C and reconstitution in 0.1% FA for MS analysis. For ADPr MS, cells were treated with 1 mM H2O2 in PBS for 10 min at 37°C and pelleted at 4°C. Pellets were lysed in GndHCl lysis buffer, alternating vigorous shaking and vortexing, and snap frozen in liquid nitrogen. Lysates were thawed at RT, treated with 5 mM TCEP and 5 mM CAA, and sonicated on ice for 45s at 60% amplitude. Lysates were incubated with 1:200 w/w Lys-C for 3h, then overnight with 1:200 w/w Trypsin after diluting to 1.5M GndHCl with 50 mM ABC. TFA was added to samples at 0.5% v/v and precipitates were removed by centrifuging. Peptides were purified using Sep-Pak C18 cartridges, eluted in 30% ACN in 0.1% TFA, frozen overnight at -80°C, lyophilized for 96h, and reconstituted in AP buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 50 mM NaCl, 1 mM MgCl2, and 250 µM DTT). Recombinant hPARG (1:10,000 w/w) was added to each sample and incubated overnight at RT with gentle shaking to reduce ADP-ribose polymers, and precipitates were removed by centrifuging. Peptides were incubated with GST-tagged Af1521 macrodomain beads (100 µL dry beads/10 mg sample, produced in-house using BL21(DE3) bacteria) in a head-over-tail mixer at 4°C for 3h. Beads were washed 2X with cold AP buffer, 2X with cold PBS/250 µM DTT, and 2X with cold water. ADP-ribosylated peptides were eluted with 0.15% TFA and centrifuged through 0.45 µM spin filters, followed by centrifuging through 100 kDa cut-off spin filters. Peptides were purified and fractionated on stage tips at high pH into 2 fractions. After loading samples for high pH fractionation, the flowthrough was collected and subjected to low pH stage tip purification.  Proteome samples were measured on Orbitrap Exploris 480 MS using HCD fragmentation. ADPr samples were measured on Orbitrap Fusion Lumos™ Tribrid™ MS using EThcD fragmentation. Samples were analyzed on 15 – 20 cm analytical columns (75 µm internal diameter), packed in-house using ReproSil-Pur 120 C18-AQ 1.9 µm beads connected to a nanoscale EASY-nLC 1200. The analytical column was heated to 40°C and peptides were eluted with a gradient of buffer A (0.1% formic acid) and buffer B (80% ACN in 0.1% formic acid). The proteome primary gradient ranged from 5% to 35% buffer B over 50 min, followed by 10 min of ramp-up and wash-out. For high pH ADPr samples, the primary gradient ranged from 3% to 38% buffer B over 38 min, followed by 12 min of ramp-up and wash-out. For low pH ADPr samples, the primary gradient ranged from 5% to 15% buffer B over 22 min, an increase to 30% buffer B over 6 min and a 12-min ramp-up and wash out. Electrospray ionization was achieved using a NanoSpray Flex NG ion source. Spray voltage was set to 2 kV, capillary temp to 275°C, and RF level to 40%. Full scans were performed at 120K resolution with a scan range of 300–1750 m/z and maximum injection time at auto. Normalized AGC target was 200 for proteome and 150 for ADPr samples. Proteome precursors were fragmented using HCD with 25% normalized collision energy (NCE), a precursor isolation width of 1.3 m/z and normalized AGC target of 200. Top 18 precursors with charge 2–6 were isolated for MS2, with a dynamic exclusion of 60s. MS2 spectra were measured in the Orbitrap with max injection time set to auto and 15K scan resolution. ADPr precursor isolation was performed at 1.3 m/z width, normalized AGC target of 400, and EThcD fragmented with NCE of 20. Top 3 precursors with charge 3–5 were isolated for MS2 with 60s dynamic exclusion time. MS2 scan resolution was 60K, with ions collected in the orbitrap at 1000 ms max injection time.

DATA PROCESSING:
RAW MS data for proteome samples was analyzed using MaxQuant software version 2.0.3.1 with default settings unless indicated. A human fasta file downloaded on 02.03.2022 from Uniprot.org was used to generate a theoretical spectral library. Label free quantification via Fast LFQ was enabled and normalization type was set to classic. Match between runs was enabled with a match time window of 0.7 min and alignment time window of 20 min. From the proteinGroups.txt file, the data was further filtered to remove potential contaminants, reverse hits, proteins only identified by site, and proteins quantified with LFQ intensity in fewer than three replicates of at least one cell line.  RAW MS data for ADPr samples was analyzed using MaxQuant version 2.1.3.0 with default settings unless indicated. A human fasta file downloaded on 11.03.2021 from Uniprot.org was used to generate a theoretical spectral library. The maximum missed cleavages was set to 6. Methionine oxidation, N-terminal acetylation, cysteine carbamidomethylation, and ADP-ribosylation on cysteine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid, histidine, lysine, arginine, serine, threonine, and tyrosine residues were set as variable modifications, and the maximum number of modifications per peptide was set to 5. Label free quantification by fast LFQ was enabled and normalization type set to none. Match between runs was enabled with a 0.7-min. match time window and 20 min. alignment time window. Beyond the automatic filtering and FDR of 0.01 set by MaxQuant, the data was manually filtered with the statistical software Perseus(Tyanova, Temu et al. 2016) and Microsoft Excel to ensure proper identification and localization of ADP-ribosylation sites as follows: potential contaminants, reverse hits, proteins only identified by site were removed, peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) with more than one ADPr modification were excluded, and only ADPr site assignments with localization probability above 0.9 were included for site identification and above 0.75 for quantification. Because default MaxQuant intensity assignments to modification sites also include non-localized or poorly localized evidences, intensity values were manually mapped from the evidence.txt to the sites table based on localized PSMs only.
