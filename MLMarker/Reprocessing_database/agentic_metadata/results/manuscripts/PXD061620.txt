ABSTRACT:
Shotgun direct infusion mass spectrometry (DI-MS) enables high-throughput proteomics. Yet, its performance critically depends on nanospray emitter quality. We present here precision-engineered nanospray emitters with an optimized 4-degree emitter tip angle, designed for sensitive, reproducible, user friendly, and compatible across mass spectrometry platforms that demonstrated enhanced ionization efficiency and spray stability, showing a 21-fold improvement in protein identification rates compared to commercial designs. Using both digested HeLa cell protein extracts and plasma samples from Alzheimer's disease (AD) patients and controls, where the 14 most high-abundant proteins were depleted, we performed a comprehensive DI-MS protein identification and precise quantification using both Orbitrap Exploris 480 (OE480) and Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometers. We consistently identified and quantified over 160 proteins in AD plasma samples in 4 min DI-MS runs, with superior reproducibility (CV < 15%) and sensitivity on the Orbitrap Astral. Our analysis revealed several unreported disease-associated protein signatures, while confirming known AD biomarkers, with C5, C4BPA, and FAM135B as the protein combination with the highest potential as AD plasma biomarkers for the detection of the disease. The enhanced performance of these standardized engineered nanospray emitters over the established DI-MS workflow establishes a robust and scalable platform for high-throughput clinical proteomics.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Prior to mass spectrometry analysis of plasma samples by DI-MS, the 14 most abundant plasma proteins were depleted by immunoaffinity strategies using commercial resin (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A36372). In brief, 200 µL of the resin were added to the spin column (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 89868) and cleaned with 400 µL of 1x PBS and 2 min centrifugation at 500 g and 4ºC. Then, 2.5 µL of each plasma sample were incubated with the resin in 200 µL of 1x PBS for five minutes at room temperature (RT) and in rotation. After sample centrifugation at 500 g for 2 min and 4ºC, the supernatant (depleted plasma) was recovered in a new tube, and the resin and columns discarded. Depleted samples were stored at -20ºC until use. The concentration of the plasma depleted samples was measured with the NanoDrop 2000C (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Next, prior to trypsin digestion, 200 µL of each plasma sample were incubated with 8 M urea to reach a final urea concentration of 6 M for protein denaturalization. Then, samples were reduced with 10 mM TCEP for 45 min at 37ºC and 1000 rpm, and alkylated in the dark with 40 mM of chloroacetamide for 30 min at RT and 1000 rpm. Subsequently, samples were incubated with 100 µL of SeraMag magnetic beads mix (50% hydrophilic beads - 50% hydrophobic beads, Cytiva) and 50% acetonitrile (ACN) for 35 min at RT and 1000 rpm to allow protein binding to the beads. After protein anchoring, the supernatants were discarded, and the magnetic beads were washed twice with 70% ethanol and once with 100% ACN. Finally, supernatants were discarded, and plasma proteins were first digested with 1:500 LysC (Wako) at 37ºC for 3 h and 1000 rpm, followed by a second digestion with 1:20 Trypsin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) overnight (O/N) at 37ºC and 1000 rpm. Both digestions were performed in 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate (ABC) buffer pH 8.0 in a final volume of 100 µL. After trypsin digestion, samples were sonicated for 2 min, and the supernatants collected. Then, beads were sonicated again with 100 μL of 50 mM ABC buffer, to ensure peptide recovery. The two supernatants collected after sonication were pooled together, dried under vacuum, and stored at -80ºC until data independent acquisition direct injection (DIA-DI) analysis using an Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer.  Samples were injected and analyzed onto the Orbitrap Astral as above described for the OE480 mass spectrometer. For electrospray ionization (ESI), a liquid junction voltage of 2250 V and a capillary temperature of 280°C were applied. The full MS scan method employed an m/z range of 380–980, an Orbitrap resolution of 240,000 (at m/z 200), an automatic gain control (AGC) target of 500%, and a maximum injection time (IT) of 5 ms. MS/MS fragmentation data was acquired using the Astral mass analyzer, with an AGC target of 500%, an injection time of 3 ms, and a normalized collision energy (NCE) of 25 for precursor fragmentation. The scan range was set from m/z 150 to 2000, with an isolation window of 2 m/z, and window placement optimization enabled, analyzing a total of 299 windows per cycle.

DATA PROCESSING:
Raw data of plasma samples obtained with the OE480 and the Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometers were analyzed with Spectronaut (version 19.1.240724) using standardized workflows. DIA raw data and Uniprot UP000005640_9606.fasta Homo sapiens (march, 2024) database (20,418 protein entries) were used for the construction of the spectral library by directDIA. Trypsin/P and Lys/P were selected as the digestion enzymes and a maximum of 2 missed cleavages were allowed. Carbamidomethylation of cysteines was set as a fixed modification, and methionine oxidation and N-terminal acetylation were set as variable modifications. For DIA analysis, a standard workflow was used. The maximum FDR for peptide spectral match (PSM), peptide, and protein identifications were set at 0.01, automatic cross-run normalization was enabled, and all identified peptides were used for protein quantification. Protein inference was performed using the IDPicker algorithm. Non imputation was performed during peptide and protein identification and quantification with Spectronaut.
