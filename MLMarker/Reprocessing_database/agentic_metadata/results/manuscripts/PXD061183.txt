ABSTRACT:
Understanding thyroid aging is essential for deciphering its physiological and pathological alterations, yet the causal relationship between aging and thyroid disease remains poorly characterized. We conducted a comprehensive proteomic analysis of 6109 thyroid tissues across ages 8-87 from 22 clinical centers (retrospective and prospective cohorts). Using a machine learning-based biological aging clock, we quantified accelerated aging phenotypes in pathological tissues, including benign and malignant nodules. We identified linear and non-linear age-associated proteins and pathways related to metabolism, immune response, and extracellular matrix organization remodeling, with critical transitions observed during the third, sixth, and eighth decades. Cross-species analysis of mouse and monkey tissues uncovered conserved aging markers. Intervention studies suggested that low-protein diets delay thyroid aging in mice, and therapeutic drug screening identified potential anti-tumor candidates. This study establishes the first organ-specific thyroid aging clock, linking tumorigenesis to accelerated aging, and proposing dietary interventions for thyroid longevity.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Protein extraction and digestion Dewaxed FFPE samples and red blood cell (RBC)-depleted FNA biopsies were prepared as described29. Briefly, samples were lysed in 6 M urea (Sigma-Aldrich) and 2 M thiourea (Sigma-Aldrich) using PCT program. Then, 10 mM Tris(2-carboxyethyl) phosphine hydrochloride (TCEP, Sigma-Aldrich) and 40 mM iodoacetamide (IAA, Sigma-Aldrich) were added and vortexed for 30 min in the dark. Then, LysC and trypsin (Hualishi Tech. Ltd., Beijing, China) digestion was performed by PCT program. The peptides were desalted before LC-MS analysis. DIA-MS/MS analysis Q Exactive HF  The proteome analysis was performed similar as previously described29. Peptides were separated using Ultimate 3000 or nanoLC-MS/MS system and ionized into Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific™, San Jose, USA) in data independent acquisition (DIA) mode as described29. Briefly, peptides were separated using a 45-minute LC gradient at 300 nL/min, with a linear gradient of 3%–25% buffer B (Buffer A: 2% acetonitrile, 98% H2O, 0.1% formic acid; Buffer B: 98% acetonitrile, 2% H2O, 0.1% formic acid). A full MS scan was acquired over a 390–1010 mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) range with a resolution of 60,000, automatic gain control (AGC) target of 3e6 charges, maximum injection time of 100 ms. Then, at a 30,000 resolution, 1e6 charges AGC target, 27% normalized collision energy, with the charge state set to 2, auto maximum injection time, 24 MS/MS scans were acquired (center of isolation window were 410, 430, 450, 470, 490, 510, 530, 550, 570, 590, 610, 630, 650, 670, 690, 710, 730, 770, 790, 820, 860, 910, 970 m/z.  Orbitrap Exploris 480 Peptide separation was achieved using a Thermo Fisher Scientific UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano System over a 60-minute gradient. Analysis was performed on an Orbitrap Exploris 480 mass spectrometer using FAIMS-DIA (Field Asymmetric Ion Mobility Spectrometry-Data Independent Acquisition).The effective gradient was uniformly changed from 7% to 30% of buffer B. MS1 scans were acquired over a range of 390–1010 m/z, with a resolution of 60,000. The normalized higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) collision energy was set to 32%. The resulting fragment ions were analyzed across 34 DIA windows at a resolution of 30,000. Specifically, 14 windows with a width of 15 Th were implemented at m/z of 395–605 Th, and 20 windows with a width of 20 Th were applied at m/z of 600–1000 Th. Two compensation voltages (CVs) of −68V and −48V for FAIMS ProTM. The normalized AGC target for MS1 was set at 300%, and for MS2, it was set at 2000%, with a maximum ion-injection accumulation time of 50 ms and 54 ms, respectively.

DATA PROCESSING:
DIA raw files were analyzed using DIA-NN (version 1.8.1)30 and against our thyroid-specific spectral librarys31 (QE-HF containing 136,821 precursors, 111,689 peptides, 10,547 protein groups, Orbitrap Exploris 480 containing 175,086 precursors, 152,353 peptides, 12,020 protein groups from the Swiss-Prot database of Homo sapiens). The peptide length range, precursor m/z range, and fragment ion m/z range were set as 6–30, 300–1800, and 200–1800, respectively. Different runs were treated as unrelated with disabled MBR. Precursors and proteins were determinate at 0.01 false discovery rate (FDR). Other parameters were left to default.
