ABSTRACT:
Selenium-dependent glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is the guardian of ferroptosis and prevents unrestrained (phospho)lipid peroxidation by directly reducing phospholipid hydroperoxides (PLOOH) to their corresponding alcohols. However, it remains unclear whether other phospholipid peroxidases can also contribute to ferroptosis prevention, albeit to a varying degree. Here we show that cells lacking GPX4 still exhibit substantial PLOOH reduction capacity, arguing for the presence of alternative PLOOH peroxidases. Mechanistically, we uncover that PRDX6 facilitates intracellular selenium handling, which is crucial for selenium incorporation into selenoproteins, including GPX4.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
PRDX6 dictates ferroptosis sensitivity by directing cellular selenium mobilization

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Recombinant PRDX6 was reacted with sodium selenite, reduced L-Glutathione and 4-(2-Hydroxyethyl)piperazine-1-ethane-sulfonic acid (HEPES) pH 7 in water. In control experiments, either sodium selenite or glutathione was omitted from the reaction. After alkylation by iodoacetamide (10 mM final) PRDX6 was digested with Trypsin in 40 mM HEPES pH 7 for 3 h at 37°C. Peptides were desalted by solid phase extraction using (Oasis PRiME HLB 96-well µElution plate).

DATA PROCESSING:
Data was processed with MaxQuant version 2.5.2.0 and searched against an PRDX6 aminoacidsequence. As Se modifications are commonly not detected by MaxQuant, extracted ion chromatograms were created within Skyline version 23.1.0.455 for relevantly modified PRDX6 peptdides including Se + carbamidomethyl (monoisotopic peak +136.938) and Se + glutathione (monoisotopic peak +384.9847) at cysteines.

ABSTRACT (PubMed):
Selenium-dependent glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is the guardian of ferroptosis, preventing unrestrained (phospho)lipid peroxidation by reducing phospholipid hydroperoxides (PLOOH). However, the contribution of other phospholipid peroxidases in ferroptosis protection remains unclear. We show that cells lacking GPX4 still exhibit substantial PLOOH-reducing capacity, suggesting a contribution of alternative PLOOH peroxidases. By scrutinizing potential candidates, we found that although overexpression of peroxiredoxin 6 (PRDX6), a thiol-specific antioxidant enzyme with reported PLOOH-reducing activity, failed to prevent ferroptosis, its genetic loss sensitizes cancer cells to ferroptosis. Mechanistically, we uncover that PRDX6, beyond its known peroxidase activity, acts as a selenium-acceptor protein, facilitating intracellular selenium utilization and efficient selenium incorporation into selenoproteins, including GPX4. Its physiological significance was demonstrated by reduced GPX4 expression in Prdx6-deficient mouse brains and increased sensitivity to ferroptosis in PRDX6-deficient tumor xenografts in mice. Our study highlights PRDX6 as a critical player in directing cellular selenium utilization and dictating ferroptosis sensitivity.
