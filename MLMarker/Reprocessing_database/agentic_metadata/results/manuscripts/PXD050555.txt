ABSTRACT:
We conducted a comprehensive screening of bioactive compounds sourced from natural products, specifically targeting those capable of inducing apoptosis while inhibiting tyrosine kinase activity. Our investigation encompassed a diverse range of compounds, including pure compounds, compound mixtures, and peptides, all evaluated within the A549 cell line. To delve into the underlying molecular mechanisms, we employed phosphoproteomics analysis, which enabled us to comprehensively assess the impact of these bioactive compounds on cellular pathways. Through labeling digested proteins with distinct isobaric tags, we meticulously examined the cellular response to each compound. In our experimental design, we included two established chemical drugs, Afatinib and Osimertinib, serving as positive controls. These drugs, both kinase inhibitors utilized in cancer treatment, operate via distinct mechanisms and target different kinases. By comparing the effects of our test compounds with these controls, we aimed to elucidate their potential therapeutic relevance and mechanisms of action. Among the compounds examined were extracts from Phallus indusiatus and Fomes rimosus (Berk.) Cooke, as well as specific compounds like Chamuangone, Cannabigerol (CBG), Cannabidiol (CBD), and NP1-cyclic peptide

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
A549 cells in various conditions were lysed using a lysis buffer solution, comprising 0.5% TritonX-100, 2mM TCEP, 50 mM HEPES-KOH (pH 8.2), and supplemented with a protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail. The lysed tissue was subjected to sonication at 80% amplitude, alternating 2 seconds on and 8 seconds off for a total of three cycles (30 seconds in total). The protein solution was collected by centrifugation at 14,000g for 20 minutes at 12 C. For phosphorprotein enrichment analysis, we used 3 mg. at concentration of 5 g/L for the enrichment. The phophorprotein enrichment was conducted by Pierce® Phosphoprotein Enrichment Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The protein concentration was adjusted to 1 ug/uL by diluting the protein solution with binding/washing reagent from to final concentration 1 g/L and followed the instructor available in the kit. The elution fraction of phosphoproteins was desalting based on size-exclusion chromatography by Zeba™ Spin Desalting Columns (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). After that, the protein concentration of the flow-through fraction was measured and adjust to 0.6 g/L. A total of 60 g protein was subjected to gel-free based digestion using previous protocol with minor modificaiton. Briefly, the protein solution was precipitated using ice-cold acetone at 1:10 (v/v) ratio at -20C for 16 hours. Then, the samples were subjected to centrifugation at 16,000g for 15 min and the supernatants were discarded. The protein pellet was reconstituted in 0.25% RapidGest SF (Waters, UK) in 10 mM HEPES-KOH, pH 8.2 and 5 mM NaCl. The protein concentrations were determined by the bicinchoninic acid assay kit (Pierce, New York, NY, USA), using bovine serum albumin as the standard. For the protein digestion and clean-up, the protein amount of 30 µg was subjected to gel-free-based digestion method. Reduction of the sulfhydryl bonds by using 2 mM TCEP in 10 mM HEPES-KOH, pH 8.2 at 60C for 1 hour and alkylation of sulfhydryl by 10 mM IAA in 10 mM HEPES-KOH, pH 8.2 at room temperature for 35 minutes in the dark. The solution was cleaned up by the Desalting column (Zebra-spin). The flow-through solution was enzymatically digested by trypsin (Promega, Germany) at a ratio of 1:50 (enzyme: protein) and incubated at 38C for 3 hours. The reaction was terminated by incubation at 90C for 15 minutes. The supernatant was collected by centrifugation at 16,000g for 20 minutes. The tryptic peptides were dried and reconstituted in 25 μL of 100 mM HEPES-KOH, pH 8.2 prior to labeling. Peptide concentration is measured using peptide quantification kit (Pierce™ Quantitative Peptide Assays & Standards). The experiment was conducted in three independently (n=3). For TMT labeling (after reconstituting each tube with 41 L of acetonitrile), all of phosphopeptides from each sample were labeled using TMT10plex™ Label reagent set reaction for 1 hour at room temperature.
