ABSTRACT:
The cell seretome is diverse and contains extracellular secreted vesicles (EVs) as well as non-vesicular extracellular nanoparticles (NVEPs) called exomeres and supermeres, all of which carry different kinds of cell signaling cargos. We have developed new techniques to purify exomeres and supermeres by ultracentrifugation (UC) and by fast protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) from cell conditioned media and from blood plasma. We utilized a proteomics approach to compare UC and FPLC methods with standard UC and density gradient methods of isolating EVs and new methods of isolating NVEPs. We acquired LC-MS/MS data from conditioned media using standard 2D plastic and hollow fiber 3D bioreactor production from a CRC cell line, DiFi, using all these methods and growth culture conditions.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Proteome of DiFi EVs, exomeres and supermeres purified by ultracentrifugation and FPLC

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
DiFi cells were cultured until 80% confluent, washed 3X with PBS, and cultured in serum-free medium for 48 h prior to collection of EVs and NVEPs. Hollow fiber bioreactors were loaded with 1-5 x 108 DiFi cells, allowed to reach equilibrium growth based on glucose utilization, and harvested for media collection. With each harvest, 20 mL of conditioned media was centrifuged at 250g then 2,500g for 10 min each to remove cells and debris, respectively. Media was gravity filtered through a Millex 0.22-µm pore syringe filter. Four separate pools of conditioned media were created and distributed so that each pool was roughly equivalent in terms of date of isolation of material pooled. Each pool consisted of 400 mL of conditioned media. Aliquots (60 mL) were taken for UC purification, OptiPrep cushion gradient purification, and FPLC purification. Three separate 2D stocks of DiFi media (1360 mL) were harvested from 68 plates with 20 ml of media from about 5.1 x 109 cells total. Media was concentrated and used to isolate UC-purified EV pellets (EVp), exomeres, and supermeres. Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) was performed on an ATKA Purifier fast-protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) system (GE/Cytiva) with two inline Superose 6 Increase resin columns (Cytiva). Samples were filtered off-line using 0.22 µm spin-filters, and 0.5 mL of filtered samples were injected into the FPLC. For density gradient purification of sEVs, heavy EVs, and non-vesicular fractions, the conditioned 60 mL 3D-sourced aliquots were spun at 10,000g for 30 min at 4°C. The supernatant was concentrated to 30 mL with a Centricon Plus-70 centrifugal filter. The concentrate was overlaid onto a 2 mL cushion of 60% iodixanol (OptiPrep) and centrifuged at 100,000g for 4 h at 4°C.  For isolation by density gradient, the 40% iodixanol layer was made by transferring the bottom 3 mL of the cushion (which includes 1 mL of collected EVs and 2 mL of 60% iodixanol) into a new tube. The 20%, 10%, and 5% iodixanol dilutions were prepared by diluting OptiPrep with 0.25 M sucrose/10 mM Tris, pH 7.5. Three mL of each iodixanol dilutions were subsequently layered on top of the 40% layer. The density gradients were spun at 100,000g for 18 h at 4°C. After this centrifugation step, 1 mL density gradient fractions were collected. Fractions 6, 7, 9. 10, and 12 were diluted in 2.5 mL PBS and centrifuged at 100,000g for 3 h 4oC.  To quantitate the size and concentration of these fractions, nanoparticle tracking analysis was performed using a Particle Metrix ZetaView PMX 110.   Samples were then prepared for proteomics analyses. First, samples were brought to a final concentration of 5% SDS, reduced with TCEP (10 mM) at 55 °C for 15 min, and alkylated with iodoacetamide (20 mM) for 30 min in the dark at room temperature. Protein samples were then prepared for S-Trap (ProtiFi) digestion per manufacturer’s instructions. Aqueous phosphoric acid was added to a final concentration of 2.5%, followed by addition of 90% methanol containing 100 mM TEAB (binding/wash buffer) at 6X the sample volume. Samples were loaded onto S-Trap micro columns and centrifuged at 4,000g. The columns were washed 4X with binding/wash buffer, and proteins were digested with trypsin in 50 mM TEAB for 1 h at 47°C. Peptides were eluted by sequential addition of 40 µL each of 50 mM TEAB, 0.2% formic acid, and 0.2% formic acid in 50% acetonitrile. Eluted peptides were dried and resuspended in aqueous 0.2% formic acid.

DATA PROCESSING:
Proteomics samples were analyzed by nanoflow LC-MS/MS using a Dionex Ultimate 3000 nanoLC and an Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). Peptides were loaded onto a C18 reverse phase capillary column and gradient-eluted at a flow rate of 350 nL/min, using a 90-min gradient. Peptides were analyzed using a data-dependent (top 20) method. The intensity threshold for MS/MS was set to 1 x 104, HCD collision energy was 30 nce, and dynamic exclusion was enabled. Data were searched with Sequest (Thermo Fisher Scientific) against a Homo sapiens database created from the UniprotKB protein database. Variable modifications included +15.9949 on Met (oxidation) and +57.0214 on Cys (carbamidomethylation). Search results were assembled in Scaffold 5.1.2 (Proteome Software) using a minimum filtering criteria of 95% peptide probability and 99% protein probability.
