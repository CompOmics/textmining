ABSTRACT:
The metabolic adaptations observed in cancer are widely recognized, yet the precise mechanisms of how these metabolic adjustments support tumor growth remain unclear. This study explores the impact of guanine nucleotide metabolism on cancer progression in ALK-driven lung cancer. We showed that GUK1 phosphorylation at Y74 augments guanine nucleotide synthesis and promotes tumor growth in ALK-driven lung cancer both in vitro and in vivo. Using 16-plex TMT-phosphoproteomics, we investigated the effect of the ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitor lorlatinib on patient-derived non-small cell lung cancer cell lines with and without GUK1 Y74F mutation, thus shedding light on signaling and metabolic pathways reliant on GUK1 phosphorylation in ALK-driven lung cancer cells.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
TMT-based phosphoproteomic analysis of ALK-positive patient-derived lung cancer cell lines with GUK1 phospho-null mutation and ALK TKI treatment

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Sample processing from ALK+ cell lines with GUK1 Y74F phospho-null mutation was performed as previously described (Navarrete-Perea, JPR 2018 https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jproteome.8b00217). Briefly, approximately 5 x 106 cells were grown for each condition (WT or GUK1 Y74F) and treated with either DMSO or lorlatinib (1 uM) for 1 hr. Cells were washed once with 1 x PBS and lysed in 1 mL of urea lysis buffer (8 M urea in 200mM EPPS pH8.5) at RT. Cell lysates were passed through a 21-guage needle for ten times and centrifuged at 20,000 x g for 15 min at RT. The cleared cell supernatant (protein extract) was subject to reduction/alkylation. Protein was reduced using 5 mM Pierce neutralized TCEP (cat #77720) for 15 min at RT, alkylated with 10 mM iodoacetamide (cat # I6125) for 30 min in dark at RT and excess iodoacetamide was quenched with 5 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) for 15 min at RT. This was followed by methanol/chloroform precipitation of 100 μg of protein, reconstitution in 200 mM EPPS pH 8.5, digestion overnight with 1:100 LysC and 1:100 trypsin at 37°C. Peptides were labelled by adding anhydrous acetonitrile to ~30%, followed by labelling with TMTpro reagent. 1% of each labeled sample was combined and analyzed unfractionated to ensure labeling efficiency was >97% and that the samples are mixed at a 1:1 (total amount) ratio across all conditions. After mixing, labelled peptide samples were de-salted using a 200 mg Sep-Pak cartridge followed by drying in a rotary evaporator. Phosphopeptides were enriched using a Fe-NTA spin column and desalted via StageTip for LC-MS/MS analysis, while the flow-through was dried, reconstituted in 5% ACN 10mM ammonium bicarbonate, and fractionated using basic pH reverse phase chromatography on an Agilent 300extend-C18 column (3.5 μm, 4.6x250 mm) using an Agilent Infinity 1260 HPLC. Peptides were subjected to a 75 min linear gradient from 13% to 42% of Buffer B (10 mM ammonium bicarbonate, 90% ACN, pH 8) at a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min, resulting in a total of 96 fractions which were consolidated into 24 by combining (in a chessboard pattern) four alternating wells down columns of the 96-well plate. Each combined fraction was desalted via StageTip for LC-MS/MS analysis.  Mass spectrometry data were collected using an Orbitrap Fusion Lumos mass spectrometer coupled to a Proxeon EASY-nLC 1200 LC pump (both from ThermoFisher Scientific). Peptides were separated over a 1.5-hour (proteome) or 2.5-hour (phosphopeptide-enriched) gradient of 4 to 25% acetonitrile in 0.125% formic acid at a flow rate of 525 nL/min on a 35 cm long 100 μm inner diameter column packed with Accucore (2.6 μm, 150Å) resin (ThermoFisher Scientific). The 10 most intense MS1 ions were selected for MS2 analysis. Data were acquired with a FAIMS device installed; protein samples used CV’s of -40, -60, and -80 while phosphor-enriched samples used CV’s of -30, -50, -70. MS1 data were collected using the Orbitrap (60,000 resolution; maximum injection time 50 ms; AGC 4e5, 400-1600 m/z), followed by HCD fragmentation and an Orbitrap MS2 scan (50,000 resolution; maximum injection time 120 ms; AGC 1e5; 110 m/z first mass). Determined charge states between 2 and 5 were required for sequencing, and a 90 s dynamic exclusion window was applied.

DATA PROCESSING:
Mass spectra were processed using a SEQUEST-based software pipeline. Data were searched against the UniProt human database (November 2021) with a 20-ppm precursor ion tolerance for total protein-level analysis and 0.02 Da product ion tolerance. TMT tags on lysine residues and peptide N termini (+229.163 Da) and carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues (+57.021 Da) were set as static modifications, while oxidation of methionine residues (+15.995 Da) was set as a variable modification. Additionally, for phosphopeptide analysis, deamidation (+0.984 Da) of asparagine and glutamine, as well as phosphorylation (+79.966 Da) on serine, threonine, and tyrosine, were included as variable modifications. Peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) were identified, quantified, and filtered to a 1% peptide false discovery rate (FDR) and then collapsed further to a final protein-level FDR of 1%. Proteins were quantified by summing reporter ion counts across all matching PSMs. A 0.003 Da (3 millidalton) window around the theoretical m/z of each reporter ion was scanned, and the maximum intensity closest to the theoretical m/z was used. Reporter ion intensities were adjusted to correct for the isotopic impurities of the different TMT reagents according to manufacturer specifications and normalized to normalize ratios across labeling channels. Finally, for each protein, the signal-to-noise (S:N) measurements of the peptides were summed and normalized to 100 across all samples, resulting in a relative abundance (TMT RA) measurement.
