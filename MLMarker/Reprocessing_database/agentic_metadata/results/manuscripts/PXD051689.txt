ABSTRACT:
Identification of hPTM binder through the chemoproteomic approach

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
ENL reads histone β-hydroxybutyrylation to modulate gene transcription

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
hPTM probes were prepared and incubated with cell lysate, and subsequently subjected to UV irradiation by which crosslinking between probe and its targets could occur. After removing nonspecific binders by stringent washing, the captured proteins were enriched with centrifugation and digested on-bead trypsinization, followed by detection with HPLC-MS/MS to quantify protein abundance.

DATA PROCESSING:
We performed three biological replicates to reliably identify binders enriched by probe, and proteins with a maximal mean of <2.0 spectral counts were discarded. For the remaining proteins, we used an averaged enrichment ratio of 2.0 (n=3) as the cutoff to exclude nonspecific binding proteins. Furthermore, t-test between probe H3K4me3 and H3K4 was conducted to generate a set of proteins meeting a p-value<0.05 as high-confidence targets.
