ABSTRACT:
The pachynema progression, a crucial meiotic process, contributes to the completion of prophase I. Nevertheless, the regulation of this significant meiotic process remains poorly understood. In this study, we identified a novel testis-specific protein HSF5, which regulates pachynema progression during male meiosis in a chromatin-binding manner. Deficiency of HSF5 results in meiotic arrest and male infertility, characterized as unconventionally accumulated pachynema halting at the mid-to-late stage, with extensive spermatocyte apoptosis. Our scRNA-seq data confirmed consistent expressional alterations of certain driver genes(Sycp1, Msh4, Meiob, etc.) crucial for pachynema progression in Hsf5-/- individuals. Ulteriorly, HSF5 was revealed to primarily bind to promoter regions of such key divers by CUT&Tag analysis. Also, our results demonstrated that HSF5 biologically interacted with the chromatin-remodeling SWI/SNF complex, and it could function as a transcriptional factor for pachynema progression during meiosis. Therefore, our study underscored the importance of the chromatin-associated HSF5 for the differentiation of spermatocytes, improving the protein regulatory network of the pachynema progression.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Mouse testis Co-IP LC-MSMS for studying HSF5 function

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
80-100 mg of Hsf5+/+ and Hsf5-/- testes were separately dounced with 30 strokes and lysed in 1000 μl of lysis buffer [20 mM Tris-Cl (pH 7.4), 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.25% sodium deoxycholate, 5% glycerol, 1 mM dithiothreitol (DTT), and 1× protease inhibitor], and left on ice for 30 minutes. After centrifugation at 14,000 × g, 4°C for 10 minutes, the supernatant was precleared with protein A/G beads (Thermo Fisher) for 2 hours before incubation with HSF5 antibodies overnight. Protein A/G beads were then added and rotated for 4 hours. The beads were washed five times with cold, high-salt co-IP wash buffer [20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4), 500 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 5% glycerol, 1% NP-40, 0.25% sodium deoxycholate, 1 mM DTT, with fresh 1× protease inhibitor], rotating each time for 5 minutes at 4°C. The immunoprecipitated protein complexes were eluted off the beads into sample loading buffer. Elution samples were subjected to MS analyses at Shanghai Bioprofile Technology Co., Ltd. (China), and data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mass spectrometry was performed using a Q-Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific).

DATA PROCESSING:
Raw LC-MS/MS data files were imported into Proteome Discoverer software for subsequent analysis. A comprehensive protein sequence database, UniProt, was selected for the database search. The search parameters were configured as follows: trypsin was specified as the proteolytic enzyme, carbamidomethylation of cysteines was set as a fixed modification, and oxidation of methionine was set as a variable modification. The mass tolerance for precursor ions was set at 10 ppm, and for fragment ions, it was set at 0.02 Da, allowing a maximum of two missed cleavages.  Data-dependent acquisition (DDA) parameters included setting an intensity threshold for precursor ion selection and enabling dynamic exclusion to prevent the repeated selection of the same precursor ion within a specified time window. Peak detection and deconvolution were performed to identify m/z values and intensities of precursor and fragment ions.  The database search was executed to match MS/MS spectra to theoretical spectra from the protein sequence database. Peptide and protein identifications were made based on peptide spectrum matches (PSMs), with a false discovery rate (FDR) threshold of 1% applied to filter out low-confidence identifications.  For quantification, label-free quantification was performed by comparing precursor ion intensities across different samples. Data normalization was carried out to correct for technical variations, followed by statistical analysis to identify differentially expressed proteins or peptides.  Identified proteins and peptides were validated by cross-referencing with known protein databases and annotated with relevant biological information such as gene ontology and pathways. A comprehensive report was generated, including identified proteins/peptides, quantification results, and statistical analysis, and data were visualized using appropriate plots and charts. The processed data were exported in CSV format and stored securely for future reference and analysis.
