ABSTRACT:
To enable successful SLC function deorphanization we aimed to select a minimal number of cell lines covering expression of as many SLCs as possible. Based on a publicly available RNA-Seq dataset of 675 cell lines (Klijn C. et al, Nat. Biotechnol. 2015) we selected a set of 6 adherent human cell lines (HCT 116, Huh 7, LS180, MDA MB 468, SK MEL 28, 1321N1) cumulatively covering expression (TPM>1) of about 80% of all human SLCs. These cell lines will be used to generate SLC-knockout and SLC-overexpressing single cell clones. Additionally, Jump-In T-REx HEK293 cells will be used for generating SLC-overexpression cell lines.  In order to characterize the expressed proteome of these parental cell lines, we performed extensive proteomic profiling using liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Mass spectra were acquired on a Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Instrument performance was monitored by a quality control routine using BSA- and HeLa digest standards. Data was analyzed using Thermo Proteome Discoverer 2.3 software.  This data release consist of  (1) RAW mass spectrometry data files generated by the instrument per fraction, (2) ProteomeDiscoverer result files containing all Peptide-Spectrum-Matches (PSMs), peptide and protein grouping, scoring and confidence values, as well as abundance values from label-free-quantitation (LFQ).  As RESOLUTE will generate a significant number of protein-protein interaction data sets (AP-MS and BioID), the presented whole proteome profiling data set can serve as a reference core proteome data set, providing I) technical information on detected and expressed tryptic peptides and proteins; and II) provide valuable information on quantitative distribution of i.e. bait and prey proteins in the different cell lines. In conjunction with the other Omics data sets, like the previously published transcriptomics data, the characterization of the proteome will complement and enable valuable insight into the biological state of the investigated cell lines under default culturing conditions. Furthermore, members of the research community might apply advanced analyses with a focus out of the scope of the RESOLUTE project (e.g. PTM analysis).

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Quantitative proteome profiling of parental cell lines used in the RESOLUTE project (https://re-solute.eu).

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
1) CELL CULTURE Cell line: Huh-7; ID: CVCL_0336; Origin: JCRB; Growth media: DMEM, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: SK-MEL-28; ID: CVCL_0526; Origin: ATCC; Growth media: DMEM, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: 1321N1; ID: CVCL_0110; Origin: ECACC; Growth media: DMEM, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: LS180; ID: CVCL_0397; Origin: ECACC; Growth media: RPMI, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: MDA-MB-468; ID: CVCL_0419; Origin: MD ANDERSON; Growth media: RPMI, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: HCT 116; ID: CVCL_0291; Origin: ATCC; Growth media: McCoy’s 5A, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell line: Jump-In T-REx HEK293; ID: CVCL_0045; Origin: Thermo Fisher Scientific; Growth media: DMEM, 10 % FCS, Pen-Strep Cell lines were tested for mycoplasma in regular intervals. STR profiling was performed for all cell lines to confirm identity. Cell were grown in 10 cm dishes until confluence.  2) CELL LYSIS Cells were washed 1x with PBS and then harvested by cell scraping in PBS supplemented with cOmplete Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (Roche). Cell pellets were flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C. After thawing on ice cells pellets, cells were lysed in RIPA buffer. Lysates were additionally sonicated using a Covaris type sonicator and cleared by centrifugation at 14.000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. Protein concentration of transferred supernatant was measured using BCA protein assay (Pierce, Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.  3) PROTEIN DIGESTION & FRACTIONATION For each cell lysate, 250μg of total protein were digested with trypsin applying a modified FASP protocol published by Wisniewki et at 2009. After digestion, peptides were cleaned up and concentrated using C18 Solid Phase Extraction (SPE, MicroSpin columns, silica C 18, 3–300 μg capacity; The Nest Group). Eluted and dried peptides were reconstituted in 20mM ammonium formate, pH 10 prior to separation on a Gemini-NX C18 column (Phenomenex, Inc.) into 96 fractions using high pH RP-HPLC on an Agilent 1200 series HPLC. Solvent A consisted of 5% acetonitrile in 20 mM ammonium formate, pH 10 and solvent B of 90% acetonitrile in 20 mM ammonium formate, pH 10. Flow rate was set to 100 μL min−1 and peptides eluted from the column applying a three step gradient ranging from 0 to 100% solvent B. Collected fractions were acidified and pooled into 36 fractions in a concatenated fashion. After removal of organic solvent, each fraction was reconstituted with 10µl 5% formic acid.   4) LC-MS/MS ANALYSIS Mass spectrometry was performed on a Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, San Jose, CA) coupled to an Dionex Ultimate 3000RSLC nano system (ThermoFisher Scientific, San Jose, CA) via nanoflex source interface. Tryptic peptides were loaded onto a trap column (Pepmap 100 5μm, 5 x 0.3 mm, ThermoFisher Scientific, San Jose, CA) at a flow rate of 10 μL/min using 2% ACN and 0.1% TFA as loading buffer. After loading, the trap column was switched in-line with a 20 cm, 75 µm inner diameter analytical column (packed in-house with ReproSil-Pur 120 C18-AQ, 3 μm, Dr. Maisch, Ammerbuch-Entringen, Germany). Mobile-phase A consisted of 0.4% formic acid in water and mobile-phase B of 0.4% formic acid in a mix of 90% acetonitrile and 10% water. The flow rate was set to 230 nL/min and a 90 min gradient used (6 to 30% solvent B within 81 min, 30 to 65% solvent B within 8 min and, 65 to 100% solvent B within 1 min, 100% solvent B for 6 min before equilibrating at 6% solvent B for 18 min). Analysis was performed in a data-dependent acquisition mode.  Full MS scans were acquired with a mass range of 375 - 1650 m/z in the orbitrap at a resolution of 70,000 (at 200Th). Automatic gain control (AGC) was set to a target of 1 × 106 and a maximum injection time of 55ms. Precursor ions for MS2 analysis were selected using a Top15 dependant scan approach using a quadrupole isolation window of 1.6 Da and higher energy collision induced dissociation (HCD) at a normalized collision energy (NCE) of 38%. AGC target was set to 1 × 105 with a maximum injection time of 50 ms and an Orbitrap resolution of 17,500 (at 200 Th). Dynamic exclusion for selected ions was 60 s. A single lock mass at m/z 445.120024 was employed.   5) REPLICATION 7 cell lines in 36 fractions each; no biological or technical replicates

DATA PROCESSING:
A processing workflow based on the default label-free quantification workflow in Thermo Proteome Discoverer (version 2.3) was used to process all fractions of each cell line, consisting of the following elements with changes to default settings given in parentheses: - Spectrum Files RC (50ppm/0.02Da precursor/fragment ion mass tolerance) - Spectrum Selector (min. peak count = 10) - Sequest HT - Percolator (target FDR = 0.001) - Minora Feature Detector  Protein search database used: - contaminants_cRAP_20190304.fasta (based on cRAP version 2012) - uprpsp201902_human.fasta (based on UniProt version 201902)  A consensus workflow based on the default label-free quantification workflow in Thermo Proteome Discoverer (version 2.3) was used to integrate results from individual cell lines, consisting of the following elements with changes to default settings given in parentheses: - MSF Files - PSM Grouper - Peptide Validator (FDR = 0.001) - Peptide and Protein Filter (medium peptide confidence) - Protein Scorer - Protein Grouping - Peptide in Protein Annotation - Protein FDR Validator - Protein Annotation - Protein Marker - Feature Mapper - Precursor Ions Quantifier (unique peptides only)
