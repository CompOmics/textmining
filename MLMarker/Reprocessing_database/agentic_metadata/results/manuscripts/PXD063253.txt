ABSTRACT:
Gene fusions (GFs) are critical events in pediatric oncology, often serving as oncogenic drivers. However, fusion proteins and their derived neoantigens (GF-NEOs) remain underexplored for targeted immunotherapy. We developed ProteoFusioNEO, a computational tool for in silico translation of transcriptomic data, analyzing 5,190 pediatric cancer patients and 935 cell lines, yielding 382 and 446 fusion proteins. We highlight that GFs generate multiple translational outcomes with 97% being in-frame in patients. Fusion junctions exhibit the sequence motif [KQE][DG] which partly reflects the nature of exon–exon junctions albeit with additional hydrophilicity. Moreover, GF-NEOs abundance may be shaped by the motif, offering insights into fusion protein biology. Finally, a multipronged validation strategy using in vitro and in vivo systems confirms the GF-NEOs presentation through mass spectrometry-based proteomics and immunopeptidomics. Multiple GF-NEOs encoded by two versions of the ETV6-RUNX1 fusion were validated, paving the way for targeted immunotherapy development.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
All samples were speedvac (45°C), resuspended in 4% FA, and analyzed on an Easy-nLC 1200 with Orbitrap Eclipse™ platform and a homemade C18 column (Jupiter C18 3 μm, 300 Å, 150μm×35 cm, Phenomenex) connected to a nanoflex electrospray source. A FAIMS Pro™ interface interface was employed when indicated(2).  Proteasome digest DDA LC-MS/MS  Polypeptide digestion was done according to(1) and sample preparation according to(2). Peptides were resuspended at 50 ng/μL. For each experiment, peptides from 50 ng source protein were preloaded on a C4 trap column (EXP®2, 1.5 µL, 3 µm, Optimize Technologies) and eluted with a linear gradient 750-600 nL/min 10–50% of mobile phase B (80% ACN/0.1% FA) and mobile phase A (0.1% FA) for 60 min, wash gradient 600-750 nL/min 50–100% of mobile phase B in 1 min, held at 100% B for 9 min, then 750 nL/min mobile phase B 10% in 1 min, held for 4 min. MS1 scans: m/z range 280-1780, resolution 60000, normalized AGC target 125%, max. inj. time 128 ms. MS2 scans: max. 2 seconds, charge status 1-4, dynamic exclusion time 60 s, isolation window 1 m/z, normalized HCD collision energy stepping 24, 30 and 36%, resolution 50 000, scan range with defined first mass of m/z 110, normalized AGC 200%, max. inj. time 128 ms.  Proteome DDA FAIMS-LC-MS/MS Cell pellets of 5 million cells were collected for lysate with 8M Urea/50 mM HEPES buffer. Protein concentration was measured by Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermoscientific). Aliquot of 100 μg protein was diluted in 50mM pH 8.2 HEPES, denatured by 5mM TCEP (Thermofisher Scientific), Chloroacetamide (Sigma Aldrich) was added (final concentration 50 mM) for alkylation. Mixture was incubated at 37°C/1h/500 rpm (ThermoShaker). Digestion was performed with Lys-C (FUJIFILM Wako, enz/prot: 1/100, 3h) and Trypsin (Promega, enz/prot: 1/50, overnight) at 37°C. Samples were desalted on Sep-Pak C18 1 cc vacuum cartridges (Waters)(2). For each sample, 2 μg  of peptides were loaded on column and eluted at 600 nL/min with a linear gradient of 9–49% solvent B (80% ACN, 0.2% FA) in 120 min, then 49–99% B (0.2% FA) in 2 min and held for 11 min, then back to 9% B in 2 min. Four different m/z range were used with FAIMS CV combos (m/z 350-490, -36/-50/-64/-78v; m/z 488-595, 38/-50/-62/-74v; m/z 593-733, -32/-44/-56/-68v; m/z 731-1200 CV -25/-35/-45/-55v) for 4 inj. per sample. FAIMS/MS parameters are consensus among injections, set to Standard Resolution with static total carrier gas flow 4.4 L/min, ion transfer tube 275°C. MS1 scans: resolution 60,000, standard AGC target, auto mode for max. inj. time. MS2 scans: max. 3 s, charge 2-4, intensity threshold of 2E3, dynamic exclusion time 60s, window of 1.0 m/z, 34% HCD NCE for MS2 scans with a resolution of 50,000, normalized AGC target 200%, max. inj. time 100ms.   Immunopeptidome FAIMS-DDA/PRM LC-MS/MS  The immunoprecipitation was done as described from 100 millon cells(3). Peptides (IP 100 million cells, or 50 fmol stock solution for synthetic peptides) were reduced by 10 mM TCEP,alkylated by 40 mM 2-chloroacetamide in 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 8.2) for 30 minutes/37°C/500 rpm. Samples were desalted using C18 stage tips (The Nest Group Inc.). Peptides were resuspended at 2.5 million/μL and for each each technical replicates peptides corresponding to 20 million cells were preloaded on C4 precolumn (Optimize Technologies) and separated. Peptides were eluted with a linear gradient of 750-600 nL/min with 10–50% for mobile phase B (80% ACN/0.1% FA), mobile phase A: 0.1% FA) for 60 min, wash gradient of 600-750 nL/min with 50–100% B in 1 min, held at 100% B for 9 min, then back to 750 nL/min with 10% B in 1 min, held for 4 min. FAIMS was configured at a standard resolution (electrodes 100 °C), N2 carrier gas 1.6 L/min and 4.0 L/min for user carrier, DV set to −5,000 V with 3 MHz frequency for the high electric field, transit time 40 ms. For DDA: FAIMS transmission CVs were shifting between -24, -34, -41 and -46V. MS1 scans: m/z range 280-1780, resolution of 60,000, normalized AGC target 125%, max. inj. time 128ms. MS2 scans: max. 2 seconds, charge states 1-4, dynamic exclusion time of 60s, isolation window of 1 m/z, normalized HCD collision energy 24%, 30% and 36%, resolution of 50,000, scan range with defined first mass m/z 110, normalized AGC 200%, max. inj. time 128 ms. For PRM: 3 replicates FAIMS transmission voltages for precursor peptides optimized by CV scan tests with synthetic peptides (reduced/alkylated in 4% FA, direct infusing with syringe at 5 μL/min). DATA PROCESSING:
For all analysis Raw data was analyzed by PEAKS Xpro (version 10.6, BSI) or PEAKS 12.5 (BSI).  Proteasome digest DDA LC-MS/MS Data from the biological triplicate of proteasome digested or undigested synthetic polypeptides were analyzed to remove any peptides naturally occurring in at least one undigested control. Peptide spectrums with no integrated peak area were removed and only peptides 8-12 amino acids long containing the fusion site were kept. A database search against a custom database of GF sequences was done with mass tolerances 10 ppm for precursors and 0.01 Da for fragments, max. 3 variable modifications per peptide (oxidation (M), deamidation (NQ), and acetylation (N-term)) and a false discovery rate (FDR) of 5% for peptide identification.   Analysis of proteome and immunopeptidome by DDA FAIMS-LC-MS/MS A database search against Homo sapiens reference proteome UP000005640 merged with a custom GF sequence database was done with mass tolerance 10 ppm for precursors and 0.01 Da for fragments, a max. of 3 variable modifications per peptide (oxidation (M), deamidation (NQ), and acetylation (N-term)), a false discovery rate (FDR) of 5% for peptide identification.  Analysis of immunopeptidome by PRM Data was analyzed with Skyline119 (Version 24.1.0). Unless otherwise indicated, all experiments were done in three biological replicates.
