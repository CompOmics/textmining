ABSTRACT:
Chromatographic column selection can impact proteomic profiling, yet comparative studies remain limited. Here, we evaluate the performance of a conventional flame-pulled Accucore resin-packed capillary column and a microfabricated pillar array column (μPAC) in a sample multiplexed global proteome profiling experiment using six human cell lines prepared in triplicate as a TMTpro18-plex. Overall, the chromatography columns exhibited comparable performance. Specifically, the number and overlap of quantified peptides, as well as proteins, was similar between columns. Principal component and hierarchical clustering analyses highlighted reproducible patterns of cell line organization, while correlation analyses showed high replicate consistency across column formats. Similarity, analytical parameters like XCorr scores, signal-to-noise ratio, and peak resolution showed consistency. These findings demonstrate the potential for using robust, standardized microfluidic columns, such as μPAC, in lieu of traditional pull-tipped capillary columns without sacrificing depth or quantitative accuracy. Key advantages of μPAC include its ease of use and durability in a uniform format, although this advantage does come at a higher cost. This comparative analysis offers valuable insights into column selection for TMT-based quantitative proteomics.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Cell Lysis and Protein Processing. Each cell pellet was lysed by adding 300 µL of native lysis buffer (8 M urea, EPPS pH 8.0, supplemented with Pierce protease and phosphatase inhibitors). Homogenization was achieved by passing the sample 12 times through a 21-gauge needle (1.25 inches in length). The resulting homogenate was clarified by centrifugation at 21,000 × g for 5 minutes, and the supernatant was collected in a fresh tube. Protein content was quantified using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (ThermoFisher Scientific). Disulfide bonds were reduced with 5 mM tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine at ambient temperature for 30 minutes, followed by alkylation with 10 mM iodoacetamide for 20 minutes in the dark at room temperature. Excess iodoacetamide was neutralized with 10 mM dithiothreitol for 20 minutes in the dark at room temperature. Proteins were then precipitated using a methanol-chloroform method (Navarrete-Perea, Yu et al. 2018) prior to enzymatic digestion. The precipitated samples were resuspended in 200 mM EPPS (pH 8.5) and digested with LysC protease (100:1 protein-to-protease ratio) at room temperature for 2 hours. Trypsin was subsequently added (100:1 protein-to-protease ratio), and digestion continued at 37°C for 8 hours. TMTpro Labeling. TMTpro reagents (0.8 mg) were reconstituted in 40 µL of anhydrous acetonitrile. For labeling, 6 µL of this solution was combined with 25 µg of peptides and 7 µL of acetonitrile, yielding an approximate final concentration of 30% (v/v). After a 1-hour incubation at room temperature, the reaction was terminated by adding hydroxylamine to a final concentration of 0.3% (v/v). Labeled samples from all 18 conditions were combined in a 1:1 ratio. Approximately 300 µg of the pooled sample was dried under vacuum and purified using a C18 solid-phase extraction (SPE) column with a 100 mg capacity (Sep-Pak, Waters). Off-Line Basic pH Reversed-Phase (BPRP) Fractionation. The pooled TMTpro-labeled peptides were separated using BPRP high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Wang, Yang et al. 2011) on an Agilent 1260 system equipped with a degasser and UV detection at 220 and 280 nm. Peptides were eluted over a 50-minute linear gradient of 5% to 35% acetonitrile in 10 mM ammonium bicarbonate (pH 8) at a flow rate of 0.25 mL/min, using an Agilent ZORBAX 300Extend C18 column (3.5 µm particles, 2.1 mm ID, 250 mm length). This process yielded 96 fractions, which were pooled into 24 “super-fractions” (Paulo, O’Connell et al. 2016) by combining every 24th fraction from the 96-well plate. These super-fractions were acidified with 1% formic acid, concentrated under vacuum to near dryness, desalted using StageTips, dried again, and reconstituted in 5% acetonitrile with 5% formic acid for subsequent LC-FAIMS-MS2 analysis. Both columns are heated to 60°C.

DATA PROCESSING:
Spectra were analyzed using an in-house Comet-based pipeline. Searches were conducted against the human UniProt database, concatenated with a reversed decoy database, using a 50-ppm precursor ion tolerance and a 0.02 Da product ion tolerance to optimize sensitivity with Comet and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) (Beausoleil, Villén et al. 2006, Huttlin, Jedrychowski et al. 2010). Static modifications included TMTpro labels on lysine residues and peptide N-termini (+304.207 Da) and carbamidomethylation of cysteines (+57.021 Da), while methionine oxidation (+15.995 Da) was set as a variable modification. Peptide-spectrum matches (PSMs) were filtered to a 1% false discovery rate (FDR) (Elias and Gygi 2007, 2010) using LDA (Huttlin, Jedrychowski et al. 2010), and protein-level FDR was similarly adjusted to 1%. Protein quantification involved summing reporter ion counts across matching PSMs (McAlister, Huttlin et al. 2012), with adjustments for TMTpro isotopic impurities per manufacturer guidelines. Signal-to-noise (S/N) values were summed per protein, normalized across channels to correct for loading differences, and scaled to a relative abundance (RA) value of 100 across proteins. Data were analyzed and visualized using Microsoft Excel or R.
