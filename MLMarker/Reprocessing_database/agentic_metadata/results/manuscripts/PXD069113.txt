ABSTRACT:
Rotator cuff tears (RCT) are among the most common causes of shoulder dysfunction, frequently leading to chronic pain and impaired muscle performance. To gain deeper insight into the molecular mechanisms underlying this condition, proteomic approaches offer an unbiased means of characterizing global protein expression changes. In this study, we compared the proteomic profiles of healthy gracilis muscle and rotator cuff tear (RCT) tissue using label-free quantitative proteomics, with the aim of identifying differentially expressed proteins that may serve as biomarkers or provide mechanistic insight into disease progression.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Label-free quantitative proteomics of human healthy gracilis and diseased rotator cuff tear tendons

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Proteins were extracted from ~4 mg of tissue using pressure cycling technology in a Barocycler 2320ETX (Pressure Biosciences Inc.) for 60 cycles of 20 s at 450,000 psi followed by 10 s at atmospheric pressure at 33 °C in lysis buffer containing 4 M guanidine hydrochloride (GuHCl; Sigma-Aldrich, 50940) and 250 mM HEPES pH 7.8 (Sigma-Aldrich, 54457). The lysate was further disrupted by sonication at 4 °C (10 cycles of 30 s on/30 s off). Proteins were reduced with 200 mM tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine hydrochloride (TCEP; Thermo Fisher Scientific, PG82080) for 1 h at 37 °C with shaking, and alkylated with 400 mM chloroacetamide (CAA; Sigma-Aldrich, C0267) for 30 min at room temperature. The GuHCl concentration was then diluted to 1.2 M with HEPES, and proteins were pre-digested with LysC (Promega, VA1170) at a 1:50 enzyme-to-protein ratio for 4 h at 37 °C. Digestion was continued overnight at 37 °C with trypsin (Promega, V5111) at a 1:20 enzyme-to-protein ratio in 0.8 M GuHCl. Reactions were quenched by acidification to pH 2 with 10% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA).  Peptides were desalted on SepPak C18 cartridges (50 mg, Waters). Cartridges were preconditioned with methanol, activated with buffer B (80% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid, 19.9% water), and equilibrated with buffer A* (3% acetonitrile, 1% TFA, 96% water). Samples were loaded, washed with buffer A (0.1% acetonitrile, 99.9% water), and eluted with buffer B. The eluates were dried in a SpeedVac concentrator (Eppendorf) at 60 °C and resuspended in MS buffer (2% acetonitrile, 1% TFA, 97% water) containing indexed retention time (iRT) peptides (Biognosys).  For LC–MS/MS, 500 ng of human and mouse peptide mixtures were injected onto an EASY-nLC 1200 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to a Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Peptides were first loaded onto a 2 cm × 75 µm Acclaim PepMap100 trap column packed with 3 µm C18 beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 164946) and separated on a 50 cm × 75 µm PepMap RSLC analytical column packed with 2 µm C18 beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, ES803A). The mobile phases were solvent A (0.1% formic acid, 99.9% water; Fisher Scientific, LS118-212) and solvent B (80% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid, 19.9% water; Fisher Scientific, 15431423). Peptides were eluted at a flow rate of 250 nL/min with a gradient of 10–23% B over 85 min, 23–38% B over 30 min, 38–60% B over 10 min, followed by wash steps of 60–95% B in 5 min and a final hold at 95% B for 10 min, giving a total gradient length of 140 min.

DATA PROCESSING:
Human raw data were analyzed using MaxQuant v1.6.17 software using the integrated Andromeda search engine. The database was concatenated with one composed of all protein sequences in reversed order. Methionine and proline oxidation, as well as protein N-terminal acetylation were set as variable modifications. Peptide-spectrum matches (PSM’s) did not exceed 1% false discovery rate (FDR). Filtered PSM’s were further filtered for peptide and protein-level FDR of 1%. To determine differentially abundant proteins between diseased and control human tendons, global bioinformatics analysis was conducted on the normalized label-free intensity values. Normalized counts were imported into Omics Playground Suite (BigOmics Analytics, Switzerland) implemented within RStudio (98). trend.limma algorithm was used to estimate the log2(FC) and FDR (meta.q) of differentially abundant proteins between the two groups (99–101). Significance was assumed at an FDR of 0.05 -log10(meta.q) >1.3. Heatmap hierarchical clustering was performed at the feature level (protein)
