ABSTRACT:
TFIID is an essential basal transcription factor, crucial for RNA polymerase II (pol II) promoter recognition and transcription initiation. The TFIID complex consists of the TATA-binding protein (TBP) and 13 TBP-associated factors (TAFs) that contain intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) with currently unknown functions. Here, we show that a conserved IDR drives TAF2 condensation in nuclear speckles, independently of other TFIID subunits. Quantitative mass spectrometry analyses reveal that TAF2 exhibits close proximities to RNA-binding proteins, but that the TAF2 IDR specifically interacts with the splicing factor SRRM2. Modulation of TAF2 protein levels elicits alternative splicing (AS) events in RNAs coding for proteins involved in transcription and transmembrane transport, some of which overlap with SRRM2-regulated AS events. This study identifies an IDR of the basal transcription machinery as a molecular guide for protein partitioning into nuclear compartments, controlling protein complex composition and pre-mRNA splicing.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
For LC-MS/MS analysis of the GFP-TAF2-IDR pulldown samples, an UltiMateTM 3000 RSLCnano system was online coupled to a Q Exactive mass spectrometer (both Thermo Fisher Scientific).  The peptide mixture was washed and pre-concentrated on a PepMap C18 trapping column (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a flow rate of 30 µL/min and peptides were separated on a μPAC™ C18 pillar array column (200 cm bed length, Thermo Fisher Scientific) using a binary buffer system consisting of A (0.1% formic acid) and B (86% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid). Peptides were eluted applying a linear gradient from 6% to 10% B in 5 min at a flow rate of 0.75 µL/min, 10 to 45% B in 100 min and 45 to 99% in 8 min at a flow rate of 0.5 µL/min. For electrospray ionization of peptides, a Nanospray Flex ion source (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a liquid junction PST-HV-NFU (MS Wil, The Netherlands) and a fused silica emitter (20 µm inner diameter, 360 µm outer diameter, MicroOmics Technologies LLC) applying a source voltage of +1,800 V and an ion transfer tube temperature of 250 °C was used. Cycles of data-dependent acquisition (DDA) consisted of one MS1 survey scan (RF lens of 50%, normalized AGC target of 3e6, maximum injection time of 60 ms, m/z range of 375 to 1,700, resolution of 70,000 at 200m/z, profile mode) followed by MS2 scans (RF lens of 50%, normalized AGC target of 1e5, maximum injection time of 120 ms, resolution of 35,000,  isolation window 3 m/z, exclusion time 45 s, centroid mode) generated data-dependent on the 12 most abundant peptide signals of the survey scan (Top12) by higher-energy collision-induced dissociation (HCD) at a normalized energy of 28%.    For LC-MS/MS analysis of the pulldown samples from chromatin extracts, an UltiMate 3000 RSLCnano system was online coupled to an Exploris 480 mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). LC and electrospray settings were as described above for the Q Exactive, except for using a μPAC C18 trapping column (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a flow rate of 10 µL/min for washing and a μPAC C18 pillar array column (50 cm bed length, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for separation. Cycles of DDA consisted of: one MS1 survey scan (RF lens of 40%, normalized AGC target of 300%, maximum injection time of 25 ms, m/z range of 450 to 1,400, resolution of 120,000, profile mode) followed by MS2 scans (RF lens of 50%, normalized AGC target of 200%, maximum injection time of 25 ms, resolution of 15,000,  isolation window 1.3 m/z, exclusion time 30 s, centroid mode) generated in a Top12 method with HCD at a normalized energy of 28%.

DATA PROCESSING:
The raw data files were analyzed using Maxquant (version 2.0.2.0) searching against a human reference proteome database without isoforms from Uniprot (release 2024_02; 20,419 entries), complemented with the sequences of the GFP-TAF2 constructs, with tryptic specificity and a maximum number of two missed cleavages. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues was considered as fixed modification, oxidation of methionine and N-terminal protein acetylation were set as variable modifications. We activated label-free quantification (LFQ) , match between runs and the intensity based absolute quantification (iBAQ) algorithm. Peptide and protein identifications were filtered to a false discovery rate (FDR) of 1%.
