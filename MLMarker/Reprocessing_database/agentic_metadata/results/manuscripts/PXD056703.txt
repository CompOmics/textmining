ABSTRACT:
Tauopathies are characterized by the formation of tau protein-based neurofibrillary tangles which impede neuronal function and contribute to the pathology of highly prevalent neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s and Pick’s diseases. Despite the impact of these diseases, the underlying biology is poorly understood. Current therapeutic efforts to reduce and remove tau aggregates target a variety of cellular mechanisms including altering tau phosphorylation through kinase inhibition, stimulating autophagy and upregulating protein quality control mechanisms such as the endoplasmic reticulum associated protein degradation pathway (ERAD), however these efforts have not thus far resulted in effective therapeutic interventions. Here, we sought to identify new targets and mechanisms that might be exploited to address tauopathies, through a chemoproteomic-integrated phenotypic screen using a cellular model of tau-aggregate clearance. An optimized analogue derived from this effort induced tau aggregate clearance in both SH-SY5Y and iPSC-derived human neuron models of hTauP301L aggregation at nanomolar concentrations and led to the activation of the ERAD pathway. Chemoproteomic studies revealed interactions with several resident endoplasmic reticulum proteins containing thioredoxin domains whose activities are involved in the ERAD pathway and protein quality control. Genetic knockdown of one of these targets, protein disulfide isomerase 1 (P4HB), was found to recapitulate the tau aggregate-clearance phenotype, indicating that modulation of this protein may be of therapeutic benefit for tauopathies.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
SH-SY5Y cells were treated with serum-free DMEM (3-mL per plate) inoculated with competitor compound 2a (3.75-µL per plate, 20 mM, 25 µM final concentration) or DMSO (3.75-µL) and probe compound 2b or 2c (0.75-µL /per plate, 20 mM, 5 µM final concentration). The plates were then incubated (30 min, 37°C, 5% CO2) before being transferred to ice. Each plate was then scraped and the cells transferred to 15-mL centrifuge tubes, washed, and pelleted. Ice-cold DPBS (500-µL) was added to each centrifuge tube and the pellets were lysed by probe sonication (Branson Sonifier; 15 ms on, 40 ms off, 15 % amplitude, 1 s total on). Lysate protein concentrations were determined by Lowry assay (Bio-Rad Laboratories #5000113, #5000114) and were diluted with ice-cold DPBS to a final concentration of 2 mg/mL and volumes of 500-µL in 15- mL centrifuge tubes.   A stock solution was prepared by combining solutions of tris((1-benzyl-4-triazolyl)methyl)amine (330-µL, 1.7 mM in DMSO/t-BuOH, 1:4 v/v), tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine (110-µL, 50 mM in water), biotin-PEG4-azide (for target identification studies, 55-µL, 10 mM in DMSO) or biotin dialkoxydiphenylsilane (DADPS, for site-of-labeling or isoTOP-ABPP studies,), and copper sulfate (110-µL, 50 mM in water). A 55-µL aliquot of this stock solution was added to each cell lysate sample and the sample was incubated at ambient temperature for 1 hr with shaking. Cold MeOH/CHCl3 solution was then added (2.5-mL, 4:1 v/v), followed by DPBS (1-mL, ice cold) and the mixture was vortexed. The samples were centrifuged (5,000×g, 10 min) and the liquid phases were carefully removed to leave behind the interfacial protein disc. The protein disc was resuspended in MeOH/CHCl3 solution (1-mL, 4:1 v/v, ice-cold) by water bath sonication and centrifuged (5,000×g, 10 min). The supernatant was removed and the remaining protein pellet was resuspended by water bath sonication in MeOH/CHCl3 solution (2-mL, 4:1 v/v, ice-cold), centrifuged (5,000×g, 10 min) and the supernatant was again removed. To each sample was added urea solution (500-µL, 6M in DPBS) and a solution of sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, 10-µL, 100 mg/mL in water). A solution of TCEP (100 mM) and K2CO3 (300 mM) in DPBS (50-µL) was added to each sample. The protein pellet was resuspended by probe sonication (Branson Sonifier; 15 ms on, 40 ms off, 15 % amplitude, 1 s total on) and the resulting mixture was incubated (30 min, 37°C) with shaking. A solution of iodoacetamide (70-µL, 400 mM in DPBS) was added to each sample, which were then incubated at ambient temperature in the absence of light. A solution of SDS (130-µL, 100 mg/mL in water) was then added to each sample, followed by DPBS (5.5-mL). For each sample, Pierce™ Streptavidin Agarose beads (100-µL in suspension, Thermo Scientific™, cat. number 20353) were centrifuged (400×g, 2 min) and resuspended in DPBS (100-µL). This process was repeated two additional times. The beads were resuspended in DPBS (100-µL per sample, final suspension volume) and an aliquot (100-µL) was added to each sample. The samples were rotated at ambient temperature for 1.5 hr before the beads were pelleted by centrifugation (400×g, 2 min). The supernatant was removed and the beads were washed (resuspension followed by centrifugation, 400×g, 2 min and removal of supernatant) with SDS solution (5-mL, 2 mg/mL in DPBS), DPBS (2×5-mL), water (5-mL) and triethylammonium bicarbonate solution (TEAB, 5-mL, 100 mM in water, pH 8.5). The beads in each sample were resuspended in TEAB (500-µL, 100 mM in water, pH 8.5) and transferred to Lo-Bind 1.5-mL centrifuge tubes (Eppendorf, 022431102). A further volume of TEAB (500-µL, 100 mM in water, pH 8.5) was used to transfer any residual beads. The beads were centrifuged (400×g, 2 min) and the supernatant was removed. TEAB (2.2-mL, 100 mM in water, pH 8.5) and CaCl2 solution (20-µL, 100 mM in water) were added to a vial of sequencing grade modified trypsin (20 µg, Promega, V5111) and vortexed. An aliquot (200-µL) of this solution was added to each streptavidin bead sample and incubated (16 hr, 37°C) with shaking. Following incubation, the beads were pelleted by centrifugation and retained if required for site of labeling studies. The supernatants were transferred to new protein lo-bind 1.5-mL centrifuge tubes. The beads were washed with TEAB (100-µL, 100 mM in water, pH 8.5), pelleted and the washing was combined with the supernatant and acetonitrile (120-µL).  TMT labeling and sample fractionation performed according to manufacturers' instructions (Thermo Scientific, cat. nums. 90110, 84868)
