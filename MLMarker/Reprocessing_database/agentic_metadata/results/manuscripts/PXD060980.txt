ABSTRACT:
Oxidative stress is a well-known driver of neurodegeneration, yet it remains poorly understood how the global cysteine (Cys) proteome is remodeled in such conditions. Proteins with aberrantly modified Cys in response to oxidative stress can induce and exacerbate neurodegeneration. In this study, we induced oxidative stress by treating SH-SY5Y neuronal cells with the neurotoxin 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA). To identify proteins with Cys that had altered oxidation or post-translational modification statuses, we utilized a desthiobiotin iodoacetamide (DBIA) probe, which selectively labels Cys with unmodified and preserved thiols. We identified numerous proteins that exhibited reduced or enhanced Cys reactivity to DBIA in response to 6-OHDA-induced oxidative stress, many of which are critically involved in biological processes linked to cell stress responses (e.g., mitochondrial oxidative stress and apoptosis). Our study defines the remodeling of the Cys proteome and suggests potential Cys-mediated regulatory mechanisms under 6-OHDA-induced oxidative stress conditions. In the context of neurodegeneration and under these conditions, our findings provide a valuable resource for further exploration of the Cys proteome.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Chemoproteomic Profiling of Reactive Cysteines in Response to Oxidative Stress Induced by 6-Hydroxydopamine

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cells were treated with or without 6-OHDA (100 µM) for 1 or 6 h, respectively. Cells were lysed with 1% SDS lysis buffer containing 10 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, 2 mM MgCl2, 20 U/mL universal nuclease. Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Samples were incubated with the desthiobiotin iodoacetamide (DBIA, 500 µM) probe for 1 h, reduced with 3 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) for 20 min and alkylated with 25 mM iodoacetamide (IDA) for 30 min at room temperature (RT) in the dark. The detergents were removed by methanol/chloroform precipitation. The protein pellets were re-solubilized in 8 M urea and digested by Lys-C at a 1:100 (w/w) enzyme/protein ratio for 2 h, followed by tryptic digestion at a 1:100 (w/w) enzyme/protein ratio for overnight at RT in 2 M urea. Digested peptides containing DBIA-conjugated cysteines were desalted using Oasis HLB solid-phase extraction (SPE) cartridges (Waters) (Analytical chemistry 87, 1241-1249 2015) and approximately 100 μg of peptides from each sample were re-suspended in 200 mM HEPES, pH 8.5. The peptides were then labeled with the amine-based TMT 10-plex reagents (Thermo Fisher) for 1 h at RT. The reaction was quenched by adding 5% hydroxylamine solution before combining the labeled peptide samples into equivalent ratios. For cysteine peptide enrichment, dissolved samples were incubated with streptavidin agarose beads (50% v/v) in 1X PBS, pH 7.4, by rotating end-over-end for overnight at 4°C to enrich for TMT-labeled, DBIA-conjugated cysteine peptides. The flowthrough (non-cysteine-containing peptides) was retained towards global protein expression analysis. After incubation, beads were washed to remove nonspecific binding using the following procedure: 5 × 1 mL of 1X PBS, pH 7.4, and finally, 3 × 1 mL of HPLC-grade water. To elute cysteine-containing peptides, 500 μL of 0.1% TFA in 50% acetonitrile was added, and the beads were mixed at 1,000 rpm for 10 min at room temperature. Eluted peptides were transferred to a new tube, and the beads were additionally washed with 200 μL of 50% acetonitrile with 0.1% TFA and combined. Cysteine-containing peptides were dried to completion via SpeedVac. Non-cysteine-containing peptides were fractioned by off-line high pH HPLC; seventeen fractions were collected and lyophilized. Next, the TMT-labeled, cysteine-containing (and non-cysteine-containing) peptides were resuspended in 120 μL of 0.1% FA and were desalted using StageTips containing Empore™ C18-HD bonded silica (12 µm particle size; CDS Analytical). The TMT samples were analyzed using an Orbitrap Eclipse Tribrid mass spectrometer in-line with an UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Peptides were separated using an in-house packed capillary nanocolumn with 15 cm of C18 resin (3 μm, 200 Å; The Nest Group) using either 200 min gradients of 2–42% (whole proteome) or 240 min gradients of 2–60% (ABPP) acetonitrile in 0.1% FA per run. Eluted peptides were quantified using the SPS-MS3 method for TMT quantification. Briefly, MS1 spectra were acquired at 120-K resolving power with a maximum of 50-ms ion injection in the Orbitrap. MS2 spectra were acquired by selection of the top ten most abundant features via collisional induced dissociation in the ion trap using an automatic gain control (AGC) setting of 10K, quadrupole isolation width of 0.5 m/z and a maximum ion accumulation time of 35 ms. Then the spectra were passed into an SPS–MS3 scan performed using up to 10 b- and y-type MS2 fragment ions as precursors with an AGC of 200 K for a maximum of 86 ms, with a normalized collision energy setting of 48%.

DATA PROCESSING:
All acquired data were searched utilizing ProteomeDiscoverer v2.4 and v3.1. Spectral searches were performed using a custom FASTA-formatted database (human_uniprot_08122020) and the following parameters: 50 ppm precursor tolerance, fully tryptic peptides, fragment ion tolerance of 0.9 Da, and a modification by TMT10 (+229.1629 Da) on lysine and peptide N-termini was set as a static modification, while oxidation of methionine residues (+15.9949 Da), carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues (+57.0214 Da) and DBIA on cysteine residues (+239.1628 Da) were set as variable modifications. Peptide spectral matches were filtered to a peptide false discovery rate (FDR) of <1%. Resulting peptides were further filtered to obtain a 1% protein FDR at the entire dataset level, and proteins were collapsed into groups. Overlapping peptide sequences generated from different charge states, elution times and tryptic termini were grouped together into a single entry. A single quantitative value was reported, and only unique peptides were reported. Peptide quantification was performed by using the CoreQuant algorithm implemented in Proteome Discoverer Software (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The labeling scheme for the TMT experiments is: 126: DMSO for 1 h; 127N: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 1 h); 127C: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 1 h); 128N: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 1 h); 128C: DMSO for 1 h; 129N: DMSO for 6 h; 129C: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 6 h); 130N: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 6 h); 130C: 6-OHDA (100 µM, 6 h); 131: DMSO for 6 h. For TMT quantification, a 0.03-Th window was scanned around the theoretical m/z of each reporter ion to detect the presence of these ions. The maximum intensity of each ion was extracted, and the signal-to-noise (S/N) value of each protein is calculated by summing the reporter ion counts across all identified peptides. Reporter ion intensities were adjusted to correct for impurities during synthesis of different TMT reagents according to the manufacturer’s specifications. Peptide quantitative values were normalized so that the sum of the signal for all peptide amount in each channel was equal, to account for sample loading differences (column normalization).

METHODS:
Materials and Methods
Cell Culture
Human neuroblastoma SH‐SY5Y cells purchased from ATCC were maintained in the high‐glucose Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM; MilliporeSigma) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; MilliporeSigma) at 37°C in 5% CO2. Cells were found to be mycoplasma‐free using the e‐Myco kit (Boca Scientific).
Cell Viability Measurement
Cells were plated into 96‐well plates at densities of 1000–2000 cells/well. Two days later, cells were treated with 100 µM 6‐OHDA (MilliporeSigma) for an indicated time duration. Cell viability was measured using the CellTiter‐Glo assay (Promega) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, after incubation, room temperature CellTiter‐Glo reagent was added 1:1 to each well, and the plates were incubated at room temperature for 2 min. Luminescence was measured with the Synergy HT Multi‐Detection Microplate Reader and was normalized against control cells treated with vehicle.
Sample Preparation for Mass Spectrometry
Human neuroblastoma SH‐SY5Y cells were treated with or without 6‐OHDA (100 µM) for 1 or 6 h, respectively. Cells were lysed with 1% SDS lysis buffer containing 10 mM HEPES, pH 7.0, 2 mM MgCl2, 20 U/mL universal nuclease. Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and diluted to 1 mg/mL before 100 µg of protein was obtained for each sample. Samples were incubated with 500 µM of the desthiobiotin iodoacetamide (DBIA, CAS # 2924824‐04‐2; MedChemExpress, Catalog # HY‐150230) probe for 1 h, reduced with 3 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) for 20 min, and alkylated with 25 mM iodoacetamide (IDA) for 30 min at room temperature (RT) in the dark. The detergents were removed by methanol/chloroform precipitation. The protein pellets were re‐solubilized in 8 M urea and digested by Lys‐C at a 1:100 (w/w) enzyme/protein ratio for 2 h, followed by tryptic digestion at a 1:100 (w/w) enzyme/protein ratio for overnight at RT in 2 M urea. Digested peptides containing DBIA‐conjugated cysteines were desalted using Oasis HLB solid‐phase extraction (SPE) cartridges (Waters) and peptides from each sample were re‐suspended in 200 mM HEPES, pH 8.5 before ACN was added toward a final concentration of 30% v/v. The peptides were then labeled with 10 µL of the amine‐based TMT 10‐plex reagents (Thermo Fisher) for 1 h at RT. The reaction was quenched by adding 5% hydroxylamine solution before combining the labeled peptide samples into equivalent ratios. For cysteine peptide enrichment, dissolved samples were incubated with 100 µL of streptavidin agarose beads (50% v/v) in 1X PBS, pH 7.4, by rotating end‐over‐end for overnight at 4°C to enrich for TMT‐labeled, DBIA‐conjugated cysteine peptides. The flowthrough (non‐cysteine‐containing peptides) was retained toward global protein expression analysis. After incubation, beads were washed to remove nonspecific binding using the following procedure: 5 × 1 mL of 1X PBS, pH 7.4, and finally, 3 × 1 mL of HPLC‐grade water. To elute cysteine‐containing peptides, 500 µL of 0.1% TFA in 50% acetonitrile was added, and the beads were mixed at 1000 rpm for 10 min at room temperature. Eluted peptides were transferred to a new tube, and the beads were additionally washed with 200 µL of 50% acetonitrile with 0.1% TFA and combined. Cysteine‐containing peptides were dried to completion via SpeedVac. Non‐cysteine‐containing peptides were fractionated by off‐line high pH HPLC, as described in Table S6, where 17 fractions were collected and lyophilized. Next, the TMT‐labeled, cysteine‐containing (and non‐cysteine‐containing) peptides were resuspended in 120 µL of 0.1% FA and were desalted using StageTips containing Empore C18‐HD bonded silica (12 µm particle size; CDS Analytical).
Quantitative Proteomic Analysis by LC‐SPS‐MS3
The TMT samples were analyzed using an Orbitrap Eclipse Tribrid mass spectrometer in‐line with an UltiMate 3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Peptides were separated using an in‐house packed capillary nanocolumn with 15 cm of C18 resin (3 µm, 200 Å; The Nest Group) using either 200 min gradients of 2%–42% (whole proteome) or 240 min gradients of 2%–60% (ABPP) acetonitrile in 0.1% FA per run, unless otherwise noted. Eluted peptides were quantified using the SPS‐MS3 method for TMT quantification. Briefly, MS1 spectra were acquired at 120‐K resolving power with a maximum of 50‐ms ion injection in the Orbitrap. MS2 spectra were acquired by selection of the top ten most abundant features via collisional induced dissociation in the ion trap using an automatic gain control (AGC) setting of 10K, quadrupole isolation width of 0.5 m/z and a maximum ion accumulation time of 35 ms. Then the spectra were passed in an SPS–MS3 scan performed using up to 10 b‐ and y‐type MS2 fragment ions as precursors with an AGC of 200 K for a maximum of 86 ms, with a normalized collision energy setting of 48%.
