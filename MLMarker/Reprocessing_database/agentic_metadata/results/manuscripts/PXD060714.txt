ABSTRACT:
For H1 PTM identification, HeLa cells were exposed to 10 Gy IR or not and released for 2 min. Histones were extracted and deamidated peptides were identified by mass spectrometry (MS).  For CTPS1 phosphorylation sites identification, whole cell lysates were extracted, immunoprecipitated using anti-FLAG, and analyzed by MS.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION:
Understanding how H1 modifications influence chromatin accessibility around DSBs, to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

SAMPLE PROCESSING:
Protein Sample Preparation for LC-MS/MS Analysis Following separation by SDS-PAGE, the target protein band was excised and washed three times with ddH2O. The gel piece was then destained by adding 150 μL of destaining solution, followed by four washes with water. Subsequently, the gel piece was washed twice with 300 μL each of 25 mM ammonium bicarbonate, 50% acetonitrile (ACN), and 100% ACN, respectively, until the gel piece turned white due to dehydration. For reduction, 50 μL of 10 mM DTT solution was added, and the sample was incubated at 56°C for 30 minutes. After cooling to room temperature, an equal volume of 50 mM iodoacetamide (IAA) solution was added, and the sample was alkylated in the dark for 15 minutes. The gel piece was then washed again twice with 300 μL each of 25 mM ammonium bicarbonate, 50% ACN, and 100% ACN, followed by dehydration until the gel piece turned white. For enzymatic digestion, 15-20 μL of 0.01 μg/μL proteomics-grade trypsin was added, and the gel piece was allowed to swell on ice until it became transparent. Then, 30-40 μL of 50 mM NH4HCO3 containing 10% ACN was added to cover the gel piece, and the sample was digested overnight at 37°C. After digestion, the supernatant was transferred to a new EP tube. The remaining gel piece was extracted with 100 μL of extraction buffer (67% ACN, 2% formic acid) at 37°C for 30 minutes, followed by sonication for 15 minutes. The supernatant was collected, combined with the previous supernatant, and concentrated by centrifugation and drying for subsequent mass spectrometry analysis. LC-MS/MS Analysis The dried peptide sample was reconstituted in Nano-LC mobile phase A (0.1% formic acid in water) and loaded onto a nanoViper C18 pre-column (3 μm, 100 Å) for desalting with a 20 μL wash. The liquid chromatography system used was a Vanquish NEO nanoflow LC system (ThermoFisher, USA). After desalting on the pre-column, the peptides were separated on an analytical column (Acclaim PepMap RSLC, 75 μm × 25 cm, C18, 2 μm, 100 Å) using a 60-minute gradient from 6% to 44% mobile phase B (80% ACN, 0.1% formic acid). Mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a ThermoFisher Orbitrap Eclipse system (ThermoFisher, USA) equipped with a Nano Flex nano-electrospray ion source (ThermoFisher, USA). The spray voltage was set to 2 kV, and the ion transfer tube temperature was maintained at 300°C. The mass spectrometer was operated in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode, with a full MS scan resolution of 60,000 over a range of 350-1500 m/z and a maximum injection time of 118 ms. Each DDA cycle lasted 2 seconds, with a maximum injection time of 22 ms for MS/MS scans. The collision energy for higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) was set to 30% for all precursor ions, and dynamic exclusion was set to 70 seconds.

DATA PROCESSING:
The raw data files collected from mass spectrometry were processed and analyzed using PEAKS Studio 8.5 (Bioinformatics Solutions Inc., Waterloo, Canada). The database used was the target protein sp|P16403|H12_HUMAN downloaded from Uniprot. The search parameters were set as follows: trypsin digestion, precursor mass tolerance of 10 ppm, fragment mass tolerance of 0.05 Da, and a maximum of four missed cleavages. The fixed modification was cysteine carbamidomethylation. The variable modifications included protein N-terminal acetylation (Acetylation (Protein N-term)), asparagine/glutamine deamidation (Deamidation (NQ)), and methionine oxidation (Oxidation (M)), lysine acetylation (Acetylation (K)), Pyro-glu from E, Pyro-glu from Q.
